A support dashboard says the team accepted fewer new requests than it resolved. The pending count hardly moved. Before asking people to work faster, check whether the dashboard is counting every movement that enters or leaves the same stock.

This workshop audits one support service over a working week. You will reconcile the count, widen the boundary from one team to the whole service and use the result to assess a target. The numbers are a teaching dataset, rather than a report from a particular company.

## Fix the boundary and the clock

<MCQ

  pretest

  options={[

    { text: 'This team’s opening count and the whole service’s later completions.' },
    { text: 'A current snapshot and last month’s average arrival rate by themselves.' },
    { text: 'The most recent totals even if their filters and cutoff times differ.' }
  ]}
  explanation="The identity only reconciles one defined stock across one defined period. Different teams, states or clocks describe different quantities even when each report is individually correct."
/>

The stock is **the number of unfinished requests assigned to Team A at a particular instant**. Each request has an identifier and belongs to exactly one of two teams, A or B. A request marked resolved leaves this stock; reopening it brings it back. Requests waiting to be started are included, so this stock is not automatically the same as work in progress. The [Kanban Guide](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/) defines work in progress using explicit started and finished points; your measurement must likewise say which states it includes.

Take an opening snapshot before Monday's first work event and a closing snapshot after Friday's last included event. The service operates five eight-hour workdays, with no events outside those hours in this case. Use the same time zone and cutoff convention for both teams and all event exports. A transfer from A to B is recorded as one exit from A and one entry to B at the same effective time.

For this dataset, there are no deletions, merges, cancellations or unassigned requests. Real systems may have those movements; add them if they cross your chosen boundary rather than forcing them into “resolved”.

| Measurement | Team A | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Opening stock | 40 requests | Unfinished and assigned to A before the period |
| Closing stock | 38 requests | Unfinished and assigned to A after the period |
| Observation period | 5 workdays | The same period for every movement below |

### When to use this setup

Use it before comparing dashboards, evaluating a handover or deciding whether a queue is shrinking. Freeze the definition and retain the snapshots: a report filtered by today's owner can allocate old events differently from a history of who owned the request when each event occurred. [Zendesk's reporting documentation](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4944328779162-Understanding-discrepancies-between-Explore-reports-and-exports) illustrates how reopens and later group changes can change historical report results. The ledger here uses event-time ownership and actual state transitions.

## Reconstruct the missing movements

<MCQ
  client:visible
  pretest
  question="The opening count, new requests and resolutions do not reproduce the closing snapshot. What should happen next?"
  options={[
    { text: 'Check omitted state changes, transfers and measurement definitions.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Rewrite the closing snapshot so the abbreviated calculation matches.' },
    { text: 'Treat all unexplained change as new demand without checking records.' },
    { text: 'Discard the opening stock because only current activity matters.' }
  ]}
  explanation="A mismatch identifies something to investigate. It does not tell you which record or definition is wrong, nor justify inventing an adjustment."
/>

The abbreviated dashboard includes **24 newly accepted requests** and **28 resolution events**. That gives 40 + 24 − 28 = **36**, but the closing snapshot contains **38**. The **reconciliation residual**—observed closing stock minus the stock predicted by the recorded movements—is 38 − 36 = **+2 requests**.

Inspecting the event log reveals reopens and transfers. Count entries and exits during the same week:

| Movement at Team A's boundary | Count during the week | Sign in the stock equation |
|---|---|---|
| Newly accepted requests from outside the service | 24 | Add |
| Resolved requests reopened into A | 6 | Add |
| Unfinished requests transferred from B to A | 4 | Add |
| Unfinished requests marked resolved | 28 | Subtract |
| Unfinished requests transferred from A to B | 8 | Subtract |

Total entries are 24 + 6 + 4 = **34**. Total exits are 28 + 8 = **36**. Net movement is 34 − 36 = **−2 requests**, so:

**Closing stock = 40 + 24 + 6 + 4 − 28 − 8 = 38 requests**

The omitted movements contribute 6 + 4 − 8 = **+2**, exactly accounting for the abbreviated calculation's residual. The full calculation now matches the snapshot, giving a residual of zero. That is a consistency check on these records, not proof that every request was correctly resolved or every event recorded correctly; errors can offset each other.

## Follow repeated work and internal transfers

<MCQ
  client:visible
  pretest
  question="A request is marked resolved, reopened and resolved again during the week. How should an event-based stock ledger handle it?"
  options={[
    { text: 'Count each actual crossing and keep distinct-request measures separate.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Count the identifier only once and use that number for every metric.' },
    { text: 'Ignore the reopening because the request was not newly created.' },
    { text: 'Call the reopening new external demand and also count it as a reopen.' }
  ]}
  explanation="One identifier can cross the stock boundary more than once. The ledger records the transitions that change membership; a distinct-request count answers another question."
/>

Suppose request R is unfinished at Monday's opening snapshot, is marked resolved on Tuesday, reopened on Wednesday and resolved again on Friday. Follow its membership:

| Event or snapshot | Membership of the unfinished stock | Ledger contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Opening snapshot | Present | Included in the opening stock |
| Tuesday resolution | Leaves | −1 |
| Wednesday reopening | Enters | +1 |
| Friday resolution | Leaves | −1 |
| Closing snapshot | Absent | No longer in the closing stock |

For R, the movement total is −1 + 1 − 1 = **−1**. Its two resolution events and one reopen correctly remove one initially unfinished request by the end. Counting only the final resolution while retaining the reopen would add a false net zero. Counting the reopen twice, once as a new external request and once as rework, would also break the balance.

### Widen the boundary

Now include Team B. Both teams use the same request identifiers, state definitions and observation period. Every unfinished request belongs to exactly one of them.

| Measurement or movement | Team A | Team B | Whole service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening stock | 40 | 25 | 65 |
| New external requests | 24 | 10 | 34 |
| Reopens | 6 | 2 | 8 |
| Transfers received from the other team | 4 | 8 | Internal |
| Transfers sent to the other team | 8 | 4 | Internal |
| Resolution events | 28 | 15 | 43 |
| Closing stock | 38 | 26 | 64 |

For B: 25 + 10 + 2 + 8 − 15 − 4 = **26**. At service level, the same transfers appear once as an exit and once as an entry, so they cancel. They do not cross the service boundary:

**Service closing stock = 65 + 34 + 8 − 43 = 64 requests**

This is the same accounting principle used in the [Census Bureau's demographic balancing equation](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/programs-surveys/international-programs/select-topics-in-international-population-health/population-estimates-and-projections.pdf): movements within a wider boundary do not add or remove members of that wider stock. The support-service example uses its own stated membership rules.

## Convert totals to rates over the same period

<MCQ
  client:visible
  pretest
  question="A report gives arrivals during the week and a resolution rate per workday. What must happen before you subtract them?"
  options={[
    { text: 'Convert them to compatible totals or rates over the same period.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Subtract the displayed numbers because both refer to requests.' },
    { text: 'Multiply the opening stock by the arrival rate to align the units.' },
    { text: 'Treat a workday average as the number arriving on every actual day.' }
  ]}
  explanation="A period total and a rate have different units. The stock equation needs accumulated movements over the period; comparable mean rates can also be multiplied by their matching duration."
/>

All Team A movements above are **counts over five workdays**. Divide by five to obtain mean rates per staffed workday. These are averages over this observation window, not claims that each day looked alike.

| Team A movement | Weekly count | Mean per workday |
|---|---|---|
| New external requests | 24 | 24 ÷ 5 = 4.8 |
| Reopens | 6 | 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2 |
| Transfers received | 4 | 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8 |
| Resolution events | 28 | 28 ÷ 5 = 5.6 |
| Transfers sent | 8 | 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 |
| All entries | 34 | 6.8 |
| All exits | 36 | 7.2 |

Net mean rate is 6.8 − 7.2 = **−0.4 requests per workday**. Multiplying by the period gives −0.4 × 5 = **−2 requests**, the same stock change as the count ledger. The fractional mean does not imply fractions of individual requests arriving; it summarises whole events spread across days.

### Pitfall and use

You cannot combine this five-workday mean with a duration measured in calendar days without a conversion. Nor does the mean show whether a large queue formed midweek. Use event timestamps or daily snapshots when the decision concerns waiting, peak load or a promised response time. Use the period totals when reconciling the start and end.

## Choose the measurement that changes the decision

<MCQ
  client:visible
  pretest
  question="Management wants the whole-service pending count to fall faster. Which result from this audit directly tests progress toward that goal?"
  options={[
    { text: 'The service-level balance including new work, reopens and resolutions.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Team A’s outgoing transfers, treated as if they were service resolutions.' },
    { text: 'The total number of resolution events without counting entries or rework.' },
    { text: 'The new-request count alone, without the opening or closing stock.' }
  ]}
  explanation="The goal concerns the wider unfinished stock. Its change depends on all entries and exits at that boundary, while internal handovers do not reduce it."
/>

Suppose the next target is to reduce the service stock by **10 requests in one comparable week**. If new external requests and reopens repeat their previous totals, entries will be 34 + 8 = **42**. With no other boundary crossings, required resolution events are:

**Required exits = entries + desired reduction = 42 + 10 = 52 events**

| Next-week scenario | Opening stock | Entries | Resolution events | Closing stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repeat the observed resolution count | 64 | 42 | 43 | 64 + 42 − 43 = 63 |
| Reach the stated reduction target | 64 | 42 | 52 | 64 + 42 − 52 = 54 |

The arithmetic gap is 52 − 43 = **9 additional resolution events**, conditional on those entry assumptions. This is a required balance, not a measured capacity or a promise. Work difficulty, staff time, repeat resolutions and demand variation may change what is achievable. Marking requests resolved prematurely may increase reopens, invalidating the fixed-entry assumption.

Choose the next measurement according to the decision:

| Decision | Additional evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Why does finished work return? | Request-linked reopen reasons and the time since the earlier resolution |
| Can the team handle the required workload? | Work mix, handling effort, available staff time and unresolved dependencies |
| Are customers waiting too long despite a balanced count? | Request age, waiting-time distribution and timestamps within the week |
| Do exports still disagree? | Event identifiers, event-time ownership, state definitions and matching cutoffs |

A balanced ledger cannot establish service quality or identify the cause of rework. It tells you which quantity needs explaining and prevents an internal transfer or a renamed metric from standing in for the goal.
