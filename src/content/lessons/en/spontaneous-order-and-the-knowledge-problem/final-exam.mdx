This is the final exam for **Spontaneous Order & the Knowledge Problem.** It pulls
together the whole course: Hayek’s question about the availability and use of dispersed information, prices as additional information and the distinction between checking plans and explaining coordination, *I,
Pencil* and what the invisible hand actually claims, the gallery of undesigned orders
from desire paths to POW-camp cigarettes, and the failure modes — commons tragedies,
unjust outcomes, the fatal conceit, and its "let it emerge" mirror. The workshop adds local cost comparisons and an audit of compatible cases. Take your time and reason each one through.

## Data for the price-signal questions

The **transfer card** and **expanded-catalogue card** below refer only to one constructed session with fictional credits cr. A and B can each make one output valued at 20 cr by its own workshop, or make nothing for zero net benefit. They take the quote as given. A needs one tin unit plus 2 cr of other resources, or a complete substitute costing 9 cr. B needs two tin units plus 1 cr of other resources; its complete substitute appears in the catalogue. Each of three suppliers can offer one identical unit when the quote exceeds its opportunity cost. Budgets cover the choices, all units are compatible and there are no other costs or effects.

### Transfer card

Use case T at the quotation in the table. Compare B’s complete tin method, complete substitute and outside option. Private net benefit means its output value minus its own complete cost, not the aggregate account.

### Expanded-catalogue card

In case D only, user C values each of up to two units at 8 cr, has no other cost and wants both at a lower quote. No probabilities are assigned to cases. The report to audit states a quotation of 6 cr and compatible planned quantity of three units; it does not assert completed trades. The aggregate metric is the equal-credit-weight sum of included participants’ net benefits, equivalently output/use values minus consumed-resource opportunity costs.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
The knowledge problem asks how dispersed information can be used. A quotation can help people compare local alternatives without revealing its original cause. Checking compatible plans does not establish how a price was discovered or which institutional arrangement works best. Cooperation can arise without a comprehensive designer — pencils, languages, common law, POW-camp cigarette money — grown orders
(cosmos) held together by general end-independent rules, with firms as small designed
islands inside them. But spontaneous never means optimal or just: commons tragedies
and congestion are emergent too, prices reward scarcity rather than virtue, and the
fatal conceit has a lazy mirror in "just let it emerge." The practical task is to identify local decisions, missing information and the conditions and evidence needed to compare alternatives.
</Callout>
