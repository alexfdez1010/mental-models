A higher input price arrives in two workshops. One keeps its method; the other changes materials. Both have read the same quotation. Their disagreement is not evidence that one missed the message: they have different ways to make the product.

A fare works like this too. Knowing the fare helps only alongside your destination, routes and alternatives. The analogy concerns the information needed for a choice; it does not explain who set the fare or how a market price forms.

This workshop separates two jobs: **a user chooses with local information**, while **an auditor asks which records fit a report**. We will give the auditor a small, declared catalogue and test what happens when it expands. That is a teaching device, not a claim that a real economy’s knowledge is available in one spreadsheet.

Consider **one constructed session** with a homogeneous input called tin and fictional credits **cr**. Each workshop A or B can make at most one output that it personally values at **20 cr**. It can use tin, use a complete substitute method or make nothing. Making nothing gives a net benefit of zero. We are not assuming a sale to an unlisted final buyer.

**Private net benefit** means the workshop’s output value minus all its own costs for the chosen method. Both workshops have enough budget for the listed choices. They take the quotation as given: they choose a method, not a price. Costs are avoidable opportunity costs in this session; there are no omitted fees, waiting costs, quality differences or external effects.

For a quotation *p*, A’s tin method costs **p + 2** and B’s costs **2p + 1**, in cr. The substitute costs already include everything that method uses; they contain no additional tin demand. A purchase plan assumes the input can be obtained at the quoted terms. A later check must establish whether everyone’s plans can fit together. An incompatible quotation is not a record of completed production or realised benefits.

### When the quotation helps

A user can make this comparison without identifying the original distant change when the quoted terms, its own methods and their availability are all it needs for this session. If delivery risk, duration or quality affects those methods, those facts belong in the comparison too. The price does not excuse leaving them out.

Keep A and B’s initial methods fixed and inspect three records. They describe different conditions, not equally probable worlds.

At R’s reported quotation, the three supplier units can meet A’s one-unit plan and B’s two-unit plan. At S’s reported quotation, only the first supplier offers a unit and only A wants one. At D’s reported quotation, all suppliers offer, while A wants one and C two. The reported terms support **compatible plans** in each case: each participant’s preferred plan at those terms can be fulfilled simultaneously.

This is a static check. We have no bidding rule, meeting schedule or price-adjustment equation. Nor are these unique prices: for example, quotations strictly between **5.5 and 7 cr** also support the same strict plans in S and D. At boundary values some participants are indifferent; the quotations in our tables avoid those ties.

### When to use a compatibility check

Use it to test a stated quotation against a finite set of records. If the counts do not match, investigate fulfilment or a different set of terms. If they do match, you have not yet explained how the terms were discovered, whether contracts were executed, or whether the assumptions describe the real session.

### When a second observation matters

Before collecting more data, ask which competing records the new observation would separate. Here quantity separates S from D. Collecting the same quote again would not. Local information may also help a user act while leaving the auditor unable to reconstruct the whole record.

### When to revise the catalogue

Look for omitted technologies, budgets, availability, contract terms or users before treating an identification as unique. If an observed method disagrees with your predicted method, check those inputs and execution first. The discrepancy alone does not diagnose irrationality or deliberate deception.

## Separate the decision from the aggregate account

<MCQ
  client:visible
  className={"not-prose"}
  question={"The reported price and quantity match in the two remaining cases. What is needed before comparing their aggregate net benefits?"}
  options={[{"text":"Only the common payment total, because it already measures all gains"},{"text":"Only the number of compatible cases, used as their occurrence probabilities"},{"text":"The complete values, resource costs and chosen methods inside a declared accounting boundary","correct":true}]}
  explanation={"A shared price and quantity do not reconstruct the local methods or uses. The aggregate account needs the records that determine the stated metric."}
  pretest={true}
/>

Recall the market-audit distinction from supply and demand: payments between included participants cancel when their net benefits are added. Here define **aggregate net benefit** as the sum, with equal credit weights, of the private net benefits of A, B, C when present and all suppliers. Equivalently, subtract opportunity costs of consumed resources from the values of outputs and C’s uses. This is a declared accounting metric, not measured happiness or a verdict on need or justice.

To use realised accounts, now **stipulate completed allocations**. In D, the first supplier delivers to A and the other two to C; B uses the substitute. In T, the first delivers to A and the other two to B. The supplies are identical, so those matchings fulfil the compatible plans.

### Use the audit when a number seems to settle everything

Write the local choice and its alternatives; check fulfilment; list the records consistent with the report; then name any aggregate objective separately. This is useful when a shared price is being used to infer a hidden cause, prescribe an identical response to different users or declare an outcome efficient. The finite check answers the question you stated, under the conditions you actually supplied.
