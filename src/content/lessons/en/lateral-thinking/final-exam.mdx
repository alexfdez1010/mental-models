This is the finale. Six lessons taught you how to step sideways when the straight-ahead route jams: how to spot the hidden "of course", how to examine representations and generate candidates, when to widen the net and when to close it, and — crucially — when lateral thinking is exactly the wrong tool. Now we find out what stuck.

Twenty-four questions, one shot each. No pressure. (Some pressure.)

## Case data for this exam

This is an invented case. The club needs twelve packets with six blocks per person, in an approved layout of three blocks per face. Turning the sheet over is allowed. There are at most twelve sheets and a budget of thirty-six exercise credits (cr). Each sheet and each attempt to print a face cost one credit; a blank attempt is charged too. The four logged packets are part of the twelve: each uses one sheet and two attempts.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
Lateral thinking isn’t a personality, a licence to be weird, or a rejection of logic — it’s a **disciplined way to reach new starting points**. You question the question to drop hidden assumptions, examine representations and generate candidates and **harvest** the movement into something usable, generate wide before you converge, and — the mark of real mastery — you know when NOT to reach for it, letting careful vertical execution handle the problems that already have a method. Generate candidates and check them against requirements and evidence; those checks may justify developing, revising or rejecting them. Pass or not, you now have the toolkit. Go step sideways.
</Callout>
