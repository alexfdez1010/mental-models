A support team remembers more incidents of type A than type B. Should it conclude that most incidents are A? Before you answer, ask what reached the team and what survived the recall task. A register, a presentation and a memory list are different inventories.

This workshop audits a **constructed cohort**: a fixed set of incidents, followed through successive stages. It teaches what counts can establish. It does not simulate a person's mind or diagnose why anyone made a judgment.

## Name the quantity before judging it

Think of a shop window, its stockroom and your shopping list. The window selects from the stockroom; your list selects from what you saw. Counting your list does not inventory the stockroom. The analogy stops there: human remembering can also produce mistakes and reconstructions. We exclude those complications here so that every inclusion can be checked.

| Quantity | Operational meaning in this workshop | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | A distinct incident was presented to the team | That everyone noticed, understood or encoded it |
| Counted recall | A distinct presented incident ID appears in the validated recall list | How effortless remembering felt |
| Subjective ease | A person's reported experience of ease or difficulty | A verified incident count or objective frequency |
| Judgment | A person's stated estimate of frequency | The true composition of the register |

The **availability heuristic** estimates frequency or probability from assessed ease of retrieval. Tversky and Kahneman's account even allows a person to assess availability without actually retrieving examples. A counted list and felt ease therefore cannot be exchanged merely because both concern memory. [Tversky and Kahneman, published article, 1973](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(73)90033-9).

Schwarz and colleagues designed experiments to separate recalled content from experienced difficulty. Their results support a role for that experience under the studied conditions; they do not make ease the sole input to every judgment. [Schwarz and colleagues, published article, 1991](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/sommersemester2012/sozialekognition/schwarzblessstrack_etal.pdf).

### When this distinction matters

Use it when someone moves from “I remember many cases” to “they happen often” or “they were easy to remember.” Each claim needs the appropriate observation. A missing measure stays missing even when the story sounds convincing.

## Follow the incidents and their denominators

For one hypothetical four-week period, a complete register contains **100 distinct support incidents**. Every incident belongs to exactly one of two exclusive types, A or B. These labels carry no assumption about vividness, seriousness or cause.

Exactly the following IDs are presented and later validly recalled. A valid recall is a unique ID from the presented set: no invented memories, duplicate counts or recalls of unpresented incidents. Presentation and recall lists cover this same fixed period; validation is complete.

| Type | Registered N | Presented E | Validly recalled C |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | 8 | 6 |
| B | 90 | 9 | 3 |
| Total | 100 | 17 | 9 |

A **composition** asks what share of a stage belongs to A. Its denominator includes both types at that stage:

$10/100=1/10$, $8/(8+9)=8/17$, and $6/(6+3)=2/3$.

So A is a tenth of the register, about half the presentation, and two thirds of valid recalls. If someone substituted the recalled composition for the registered composition, the substitution would produce an overestimate here. We have not measured anyone making that substitution.

### The tempting denominator swap

“Most A were presented” and “most presented incidents were A” ask different questions. The former fixes the type and looks across stages; the latter fixes the stage and compares types. Write the denominator in words before dividing. Use this check whenever a percentage arrives without a named whole.

## Combine the filters without inventing probabilities

A sieve retains part of what enters it; a second sieve acts on what survived the first. That captures our nested lists. It does not mean that incidents were sampled randomly or independently.

For each type i, define the **realized exposure fraction** $e_i=E_i/N_i$, **conditional recall fraction** $q_i=C_i/E_i$ and **total inclusion fraction** $s_i=C_i/N_i$. With positive denominators:

$$s_i=e_iq_i,\qquad C_i=N_ie_iq_i.$$

The identity follows by cancelling E. It needs no independence assumption: q already conditions on presentation.

| Type | Exposure e | Recall among presented q | Total inclusion s | Reconstructed recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8/10=4/5 | 6/8=3/4 | 6/10=3/5 | 10 × 4/5 × 3/4=6 |
| B | 9/90=1/10 | 3/9=1/3 | 3/90=1/30 | 90 × 1/10 × 1/3=3 |

A begins with fewer incidents but has larger realized fractions at both filters. These counts do not tell us why. No attention, vividness, ease rating or personal judgment was measured.

### When to use the product

Use it to reconcile verified nested counts. Do not multiply unrelated percentages from different cohorts, definitions or periods. If no incident of a type was presented, conditional recall for that type is undefined; return to the raw counts instead of dividing by zero.

## Reconstruction has a price

Suppose an auditor knows the **exact realized inclusion fractions for this very cohort**, as well as the valid recall counts. Positive inclusion lets the auditor reverse the accounting:

$$N_A=6/(3/5)=10,\qquad N_B=3/(1/30)=90.$$

The recovered A share is $10/(10+90)=1/10$. This is a consistency check using stipulated complete-cohort information. In practice, knowing those exact fractions may already require the missing register. Recall alone has not supplied them.

An inclusion probability specified by a random sampling design is a different object. A random draw varies; the exact identities above do not promise that an inverse-weighted sample ratio is unbiased or exactly recovers an unknown population.

<Callout variant="warning" title="An unseen type can disappear from the list">
If a type has zero total inclusion, a zero recall count is compatible with a positive registered count. Division cannot recover that count. Broaden the evidence or leave the composition unresolved; an empty memory list is not proof of an empty register.
</Callout>

## Test which equality actually matters

A proposed fix gives both types the same exposure fraction. Keep the original register and conditional recall fractions, but present more B incidents:

| Type | Registered N | Presented E | Exposure E/N | Recalled C | Recall C/E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | 8 | 4/5 | 6 | 3/4 |
| B | 90 | 72 | 4/5 | 24 | 1/3 |

Now presented A is $8/80=1/10$, matching the register. Recalled A is $6/30=1/5$, still different. Equal exposure repaired the presentation composition, while unequal conditional recall changed it again.

<RateComparison client:visible
  title="Equal exposure does not settle recall"
  scenarios={[{"label":"Equal-exposure case","groups":[{"label":"A among presentations","events":8,"total":80},{"label":"A among recalls","events":6,"total":30}]}]}
/>

Equal **positive realized total inclusion** does preserve proportions in this finite accounting. If both types have the same fraction s, then

$$\frac{sN_A}{sN_A+sN_B}=\frac{N_A}{N_A+N_B}.$$

For instance, total inclusion of $1/10$ gives A=1 and B=9 recalled, preserving $1/10$. This condition is sufficient for composition in the model. It still says nothing about subjective ease or an actual frequency judgment.

## Transfer the audit to another cohort

Close the earlier solution. A second hypothetical four-week support register again contains 100 distinct incidents. You are given only these **exact realized total inclusion fractions** and valid recall counts. Exposure and conditional recall separately are not supplied.

| Type | Valid recalls C | Exact total inclusion s |
|---|---|---|
| A | 6 | 3/20 |
| B | 3 | 1/20 |

Recover both registered counts and the A composition before opening the solution. Then explain why the same recalled list size cannot identify the original composition without the filter information.

<details>
<summary>Check the reconstruction</summary>

$N_A=6/(3/20)=40$ and $N_B=3/(1/20)=60$. Thus A's original share is $40/100=2/5$, while its recalled share remains $6/9=2/3$.

The first cohort and this cohort have the same recalled counts but different registered compositions. Unknown filters permit both explanations. The recall list therefore does not identify which register produced it.

</details>

### Use the result without overclaiming

An audit should record the target period and incident definition; check unique IDs; name each denominator; compare verified stages; and separate any ease ratings and judgments. When filters are unknown, seek the register or better evidence. Frequency alone also leaves duration, severity and the cost of a response undecided.
