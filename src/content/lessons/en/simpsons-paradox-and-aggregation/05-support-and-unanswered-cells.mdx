A standardization formula needs more than a row for every group name. It needs a defined proportion in every group that receives positive reference weight. An empty cell is not a poor success result; it is a missing input to that comparison.

## Separate no observations from no successes

A new route D has this constructed record set. The success definition and window remain compatible across routine and complex groups, but no complex D attempt is supplied.

D’s observed overall proportion among its ten supplied attempts is $8/10=4/5$. That number is defined. A half-routine/half-complex **reference index** is not determined because its complex input is absent. Do not confuse those two quantities.

A supplied complex cell of $0/10$ would instead be a defined empirical fraction of zero. It would show ten observed failures, not certainty that every future complex document must fail. The distinction is between missing observations and observed zero successes.

## Bound the missing-input family

Let $q$ stand for a hypothetical completed complex proportion, anywhere in $[0,1]$. It is not an estimate extracted from the empty cell. The half-and-half family would be

$$I_D(q)=\frac12\frac45+\frac12q=\frac25+\frac12q.$$

At $q=0$, the index is $2/5=40\%$. At $q=1$, it is $9/10=90\%$. Every allowed intermediate $q$ gives an index between those endpoints. These are **sharp logical bounds** under the stated completion range: allowed endpoint completions attain both bounds. They are not confidence intervals and carry no specified coverage probability.

### When a bound is useful

Use it to show what the available inputs do and do not determine. A decision may remain unresolved across the range. A narrower range would need extra data or justified restrictions, which must be stated rather than hidden inside a default value.

## Check the zero-weight boundary

If the declared reference uses routine records only, its complex weight is zero. The reference index is then the known $4/5$. Omit the unused complex term before evaluating the sum; calculating zero times an undefined fraction is not the way to justify the result.

If the complex cell were instead supplied as $0/10$, the half-and-half index would be $2/5$ because the positive-weight complex rate would now be defined zero. The numerical value matches the lower completion bound, but the information is different: ten complex failures have actually been supplied in that alternative card.

<details>
<summary>Check the changed-reference bounds</summary>

$$I_D(q)=\frac34\frac45+\frac14q=\frac35+\frac14q,\qquad I_D\in[\tfrac35,\tfrac{17}{20}].$$

The endpoints are sixty and eighty-five percent. Both are attainable completions of this new reference family; neither is newly observed evidence.

</details>

Future use still needs an appropriate population, stable definitions, relevant performance evidence and, when working with estimates, uncertainty analysis. A complete current census answers its own descriptive question. It does not automatically become a model of every later workload.
