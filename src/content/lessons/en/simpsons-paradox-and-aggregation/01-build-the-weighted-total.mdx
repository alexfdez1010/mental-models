An overall percentage is not a vote in which every subgroup gets one ballot. It counts the underlying attempts. This lesson reconstructs that accounting without losing the original denominators.

For this fictional census the routine and complex groups do not overlap, include every attempt and use the same success definition. These conditions let you add their event counts and their eligible totals separately. Adding a per-document count to a per-hour measure would not meet the condition, even if both reports printed a percent sign.

### When to check compatibility

Check the partition whenever groups overlap, time windows differ or a numerator has silently changed definition. The algebra cannot repair inconsistent records. For this lesson the supplied census is complete and compatible, so the remaining work is exact accounting.

## Derive the weighted identity

Write group $g$’s success count as $s_g$, total as $n_g$ and within-group fraction as $r_g=s_g/n_g$. The full denominator is $N=\sum_g n_g$. With positive group denominators,

$$R=\frac{\sum_g s_g}{N}=\sum_g\frac{n_g}{N}\frac{s_g}{n_g}=\sum_g w_g r_g,\qquad w_g=\frac{n_g}{N}.$$

The weights are nonnegative and sum to one because the groups partition the records. For A:

$$R_A=\frac{10}{100}\frac9{10}+\frac{90}{100}\frac{36}{90}=\frac9{100}+\frac{36}{100}=\frac{45}{100}.$$

The routine denominator cancels only inside its own product. It does not turn A's $9/10$ into a fraction among B’s routine attempts. Keep the route, stratum and time window attached to every symbol.

## Read a contribution on the correct scale

A’s routine contribution is $9/100$, which contributes **nine percentage points** to the full success percentage. The complex contribution is $36/100$, or thirty-six percentage points. They sum to forty-five percentage points of a possible full hundred, conventionally reported as a success proportion of 45%.

This does not say that the routine group has a success proportion of 9%. Its within-group success proportion is 90%. The contributions and conditional proportions use different denominators.

## Test the equal-group shortcut

A simple mean gives $(90\%+40\%)/2=65\%$. It has assigned half the weight to each group, despite A’s actual one-tenth/nine-tenths composition. That can be a legitimate declared index, but it is not A’s observed overall fraction.

Equal group sizes are sufficient for a simple mean to reproduce the total. They are not necessary for numerical equality: equal group success proportions also make the weights irrelevant. The following separate constructed case has unequal group sizes:

Its aggregate is $(2+8)/(4+16)=1/2$, also the mean of the two half-success proportions. For two positive groups,

$$R-\frac{r_1+r_2}{2}=\frac{(n_1-n_2)(r_1-r_2)}{2(n_1+n_2)}.$$

Either equal denominators or equal within-group proportions makes this difference zero. In the main A case neither condition holds. A shortcut that happens to give the same number in another case is not a licence to change this case’s weights.

### When to use the identity

Use denominator weighting to reconstruct a recorded total. Use different weights only after naming a different comparison. The identity is bookkeeping, not a forecast or a causal model; incomplete cells and new populations require separate checks later.
