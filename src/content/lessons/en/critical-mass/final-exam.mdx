This exam checks the difference between a model’s assumptions, its calculated consequences and evidence about the world. It includes local network timing and the minimum seed budget under a stated objective.

## Reference models for the questions

These cards are stipulated mathematics, not measured adoption, clinical advice or a physical design. Keep them visible during the exam.

**Constant multiplication for questions six and seven:** $x_0>0$, $x_{t+1}=kx_t$, fixed $k\ge0$, no outside input, no population or capacity bound, unlimited time. “Events” in those items are divisible activity units; “dies out” means approaching zero, not necessarily reaching zero at a finite round. The total includes the initial wave and every later wave. Asymptotic regimes can differ at unity even though each fixed-horizon value varies continuously with $k$.

**Prescribed doubling for questions sixteen and seventeen:** the lily patch doubles daily until full coverage on day $30$. This is a stipulated sequence, not an ecological prediction. For the visibility comparison, assume coarse reporting can round tiny early coverage to zero and a reporting lag can hide recent changes. A tiny displayed count alone cannot distinguish continuing doubling from a stalled process. The last few doublings account for most of the final coverage without any late increase in the multiplier.

**Blocked-contribution model for questions nine and ten:** an abstract activity rule has $k_{\mathrm{eff}}=4(1-p)$, where $p$ is the fraction of contribution opportunities removed uniformly. Next-wave activity is $k_{\mathrm{eff}}x_t$ with no other input. Initial activity is $100$ units. This is not an infection or vaccination model.

| Removed share | Effective multiplier | Meaning under the rule |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 4 | Growth |
| 3/4 | 1 | Boundary, constant activity |
| 4/5 | 4/5 | Strict decline |

**Standard network for the network questions:** directed contacts A→B, B→C, C→A, A→D, C→D, D→E. Required active incoming contacts are one for A/B/C/E and two for D. Seeds are committed active at round zero; activation persists. Each update reads only the start-of-round active set and adds all eligible teams together. All five teams are seedable. One seed costs one planning unit. The deadline objective is all five active by the end of round two at minimum seed cost. Only an explicitly announced change modifies these rules.

**Stipulated controller:** ON at input at least $22$, OFF at input at most $20$, previous state retained between them. Compare starting OFF and rising from $19$ to $21$ with starting ON at $23$ and falling to $21$.

**Separate complete cost comparison:** prevention costs $8$ cost units. Recovery costs $3$ plus a temporary-loss cost of $2$. Both options are feasible and achieve the same required final state by the same deadline. There are no other costs, risks, losses or discounting in this constructed comparison.
