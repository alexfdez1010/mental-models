Two groups can start with the same number of participants and spread differently. One reaches the people who can activate the next group; the other gets stuck. “We need more people” may be true, but it leaves out where they are, what they need and when the result matters.

**Critical mass** names a threshold idea: enough of the relevant conditions are present for a process to sustain or change its behavior. The useful question is which conditions and which mechanism. A scalar multiplier, a local contact requirement and a historical switching threshold describe different models.

## The nuclear term has a precise home

In nuclear physics, a fission reaction can release neutrons that cause further fissions. Criticality refers to a self-sustaining chain reaction. It is part of normal reactor operation, including low-power states; the word does not mean an automatic explosion. [Idaho National Laboratory explains this distinction](https://inl.gov/nuclear-energy/reactor-criticality/), and the [Department of Energy describes the term's origin](https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/what-criticality-and-7-other-misunderstood-nuclear-energy-terms).

That origin supplies an analogy for self-sustaining activity. It does not supply a physical calculation for a crowd or a product. Nor is there a universal weight at which any object becomes a bomb. This course uses the threshold idea to ask more precise questions about a declared process.

One elementary mathematical model sets the next wave equal to a fixed multiple of the current wave. Let $x_t$ be nonnegative activity units in wave $t$, and assume $x_{t+1}=kx_t$ with a fixed nonnegative $k$, no outside input and no capacity limit. Starting at $100$ units:

| Fixed multiplier | Initial wave | Next wave | Following wave |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.8 | 100 | 80 | 64 |
| 1 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| 1.2 | 100 | 120 | 144 |

In this model, a multiplier below unity makes activity approach zero; unity holds it constant; a larger multiplier gives unbounded growth over an unlimited horizon. These are conditional mathematical results. Fractional activity units are allowed. Real people are discrete, resources finite and parameters may change; the table does not prove every real reinforcing process obeys this recurrence.

Use this model when the multiplication rule is defensible over the horizon of interest. A common error is to infer the rule from a rising total and then treat its assumptions as observations.

## A local threshold asks who can activate whom

A shared practice may depend on named contacts rather than a universal average. Picture a set of light switches with a rule about which other switches must already be on. The analogy makes the dependency visible; people are not switches and their willingness must be investigated.

In the fictional network below, each arrow contributes one incoming active contact. All teams require one except D, which requires two. Initial seeds are committed active immediately; everyone else joins at the next update if their requirement is met. Activations persist, and all teams read the same start-of-round snapshot.

A alone eventually activates everyone; E alone cannot activate anyone else, because no arrow leaves E. Both plans start with one team. The difference is the route through the network, not a change in the average requirement. The workshop later calculates every step, compares a deadline and proves a minimum seed budget.

Use a local model when contacts and individual requirements matter to the decision. Do not average away the very arrangement you need to compare. [Easley and Kleinberg's network models](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/networks-book/networks-book-ch19.pdf) develop the relationship between local thresholds, starting adopters and structure; this directed integer-count puzzle is an explicit teaching variant.

## Follow the course and check the conditions

Read each example as a claim with conditions. Identify what is counted, how one step is updated, what can change and what would disprove the prediction. A curve alone is not a causal explanation. A persistent state may be assumed by a toy model; that does not establish hysteresis in a real system.
