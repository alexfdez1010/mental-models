This exam covers calibration, proper scoring, uncertainty ranges and forecasting practice, including the audit of a fitted probability adjustment on later cases. Distinguish empirical scores, classification hits and population claims. Use the reference table for questions about the workshop case.

For the distinction between intervals for future observations and intervals for an estimated mean, see the [NIST engineering statistics handbook](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section5/pmd512.htm).

### Workshop reference

The table contains constructed complete batches. The adjustment maps the original probabilities to fitted development frequencies and is frozen before the later outcomes. The constant benchmark is **0.50**. All methods use the same cases, weights and outcome rule. Classification calls are yes at or above **0.50**.

| Batch | Group | Cases | Events | Non-events | Original probability | Adjusted probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Development | Lower | 10 | 4 | 6 | 0.20 | 0.40, fitted afterwards |
| Development | Higher | 10 | 6 | 4 | 0.80 | 0.60, fitted afterwards |
| Later | Lower | 10 | 2 | 8 | 0.20 | 0.40, frozen beforehand |
| Later | Higher | 10 | 8 | 2 | 0.80 | 0.60, frozen beforehand |

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
Calibration compares probabilities with event frequencies; classification hits depend on a declared decision threshold. Resolution concerns outcome separation across forecast groups. Sharpness concerns predictive concentration and does not use outcomes by itself.

Compare probability methods with a proper score on the same cases. The binary Brier decomposition includes outcome uncertainty, subtracts resolution and adds a reliability penalty. A constant base-rate forecast can be a useful benchmark without distinguishing cases.

Preserve issued forecasts, eligible IDs and clear outcome rules. Keep fitted replays separate, freeze candidates before later outcomes and retain adverse results. Report exact sample arithmetic alongside uncertainty about population calibration and future performance. For intervals, declare the target and intended coverage, then check later outcomes rather than treating a small coverage count or how wide a range feels as proof.
</Callout>
