## Exam probability card

Constructed model: all 1,000 ordered triples of digits from 0 to 9 are equiprobable, with mutually independent positions. Include triples beginning with zero. X: first digit below 9. Y: all three digits below 7. By definition Y is included in X; these are not rival causal hypotheses.

| Event | Outcome count | Probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| X | $9\times10\times10=900$ | $900/1,000=0.9$ |
| Y | $7\times7\times7=343$ | $343/1,000=0.343$ |

For the renaming question: A, B and C mean first, second and third digit below 9 respectively. E means “all three digits below 9”: exactly A and B and C, with 729 outcomes out of 1,000.

## Exam Hanlon card

Constructed catalogue of one hundred equally weighted cases: E is error without an intention to harm and D is an action with an intention to harm. They are exhaustive and exclusive only inside this model. S is a previously defined pattern, not a series of independent tests. The counts are stipulated, not measured frequencies of real people.

| Cause | All cases | With S | Without S |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E | 90 | 9 | 81 |
| D | 10 | 8 | 2 |

The posterior calculation uses cases compatible with S. The decision is separate: compare the two available responses by expected loss, in fictional credits and with risk neutrality. Unchanged operation loses zero under E and twenty under D. Review loses three under either cause, prevents that loss and has no other effects in the model. Use the same denominator across responses. Choosing a measure does not establish intent.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
A **razor** can guide a provisional comparison; it neither establishes an
explanation nor assigns probabilities by counting sentences. **Occam** avoids
unnecessary commitments for the purpose. A **conjunction** is included in each
part; the card’s independence justifies its product of marginal probabilities.
These rules do not rank any pair of hypotheses by assumption count. **Hanlon's razor** invites a check of an adequate error explanation before attributing an intention to harm. A possible story remains a hypothesis. A pattern can supply evidence without establishing intent; probabilities need data or a model.
When choosing a response, assess alternatives and consequences separately. More serious consequences do not make a cause more probable on their own. Keep the check open and state what the model leaves unresolved.
</Callout>
