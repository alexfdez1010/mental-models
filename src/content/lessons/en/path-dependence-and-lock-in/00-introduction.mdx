You can download a new tool in a minute. Moving years of files, training and integrations may take much longer. Today's choice starts from an inherited position, not from an empty desk. In a network, other people's installed choices can matter too: a new messaging tool is less useful if nobody you need to reach is there.

This course studies how sequences of choices can shape later possibilities. **Path dependence** concerns the influence of the route taken; **lock-in** describes persistence supported by barriers to switching. We will distinguish private switching costs from coordination among users. A persistent outcome may be useful, harmful or difficult to evaluate. Calling it “historical” does not settle which.

## The mechanism to investigate

An early lead can create its own reinforcement: more compatible users attract more tools or suppliers, making adoption more attractive for later users. This is **positive feedback**. When an option becomes more attractive as adoption grows, we call that **increasing returns to adoption**.

That mechanism can make early events consequential. It does not make the first entrant an inevitable winner, prove the winning standard inferior or establish permanent monopoly. The keyboard debate in [lesson two](/path-dependence-and-lock-in/02-the-qwerty-story) is a useful warning: explaining a possible mechanism and proving a particular harm are different jobs.

## Explore a deliberately small adoption model

The explorer below gives new adopters a preference for a platform's **quality score** and, with the slider above zero, for the platform already used by others. B has the higher fixed score in this constructed model. That score is not a measure of total social welfare.

Use **Add adopters** to see the sequence, then compare runs at different feedback strengths. **New run** changes the pseudorandom sequence while keeping the rules. **External shock** adds adopters to the trailing option; it is a particular intervention, not a model of every possible shock.

The display calls a platform “locked” after its share crosses a chosen threshold in this finite run. Crossing it is not a proof of permanence, an estimated real-world tipping point or a guarantee about the next run. At zero feedback, choices still contain the model's random variation; a quality preference is not a deterministic command.

## From a mechanism to an exit decision

Three questions organise the course. **What reinforces the installed choice?** That could include learning, compatible complements or expectations. **What evidence supports the proposed history and any claim of harm?** A vivid anecdote is not enough. **What would it actually take to change from here?** State the chooser, alternatives, horizon and transition costs.

The last question can concern one organisation even if nobody else's adoption matters. A contractual export fee creates a private switching burden without a network coordination game. Where other users' actions do matter, their incentives and feasible transitions have to enter the analysis too.

## The map of the course

Seven teaching lessons, then the final exam:

1. **What Path Dependence Really Claims** — examine the stronger historical hypothesis of contingent events amplified into durable outcomes, rather than merely observing that time passed.
2. **The QWERTY Story — and the Honest Debate** — separate the proposed mechanism from disputed evidence about a particular layout's inferiority.
3. **The Four Engines of Increasing Returns** — distinguish fixed costs, learning, network effects and expectations by the source of reinforcement.
4. **Lock-In, Switching Costs & Standards Wars** — identify transition costs, coordination problems and manufactured barriers to exit.
5. **Institutions, Careers & the Paths We Can't Leave** — examine commitments in organisations and careers, and distinguish future consequences from sunk spending.
6. **[How lock-in can break and how to test the claim](/path-dependence-and-lock-in/06-how-lock-in-breaks-and-where-the-model-lies)** — investigate five escape mechanisms without a universal quality ratio or an automatic coordination guarantee; test mechanism, persistence and harm separately.
7. **[Price an exit over time](/path-dependence-and-lock-in/07-price-an-exit-over-time)** — enumerate every short continuation, count repeated migration costs, compare horizons and ties, and distinguish an installed state from an irrecoverable invoice.

Then the **[final exam](/path-dependence-and-lock-in/final-exam)**: one question at a time; submitting locks the answer; no Back, retry or Restart. The score appears at the end, with a 70% pass threshold for this question set.

### How to study

Guess before revealing an answer, then compare your reasoning with the explanation. Change one model input at a time and say what you expect before using a control. The goal is to reproduce a comparison and recognise its boundary, rather than memorise a dramatic historical slogan.
