This exam combines the course’s historical mechanisms and debates with the continuation workshop. Separate evidence of reinforcement from a claim of harm; distinguish private switching costs from interdependent coordination; and calculate future plans from the inherited state, including every migration and the stated horizon. The numerical scenarios are constructed known-benefit models.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
A historical explanation specifies the sequence and reinforcement; a claim of harm additionally needs a feasible counterfactual. A private exit decision prices future benefits and every transition from the installed state over a common horizon. Keep productive consequences of past choices, remove common irrecoverable invoices, and retain all exact highest ties. A group’s coordination problem needs its own incentives and execution model. None of these comparisons proves a universal market winner.
</Callout>
