Suppose a frost damages a coffee harvest. At the old price, sellers can offer less coffee while buyers still want the same amount. In the standard competitive model, that mismatch creates pressure for a higher price. The observed outcome also depends on contracts, stocks, timing and the rules of the market.

**Supply and demand** organise that comparison. Demand describes what buyers are willing and able to buy at different prices. Supply describes what sellers are willing and able to offer. These are relationships across prices, not just the physical stock or the amount someone wishes they could afford.

A seller can choose a posted price. The model asks how buyers' and sellers' plans compare at that price, and which prices make the two quantities agree. It does not, by itself, establish that every planned trade is completed or that the result is fair.

## Build on opportunity cost

A buyer compares a purchase with the next-best use of their budget. A seller compares offering the good with keeping it or using the same resources elsewhere. Those **opportunity costs** help explain why the quantities people are willing to trade respond to price.

Supply and demand can help analyse a market, but observing a price alone does not identify the entire relationships or the cause of a change. Keep the model's quantities separate from a record of completed transactions. The workshop will let you check that distinction person by person.

## The course map

Six teaching lessons lead to a final exam:

1. **The Two Curves** — demand and supply as schedules, graph conventions and the role of opportunity cost.
2. **Equilibrium** — compare quantities at a price and examine shortage, surplus and the basic adjustment story.
3. **Shifts vs Movements** — distinguish a change in the good's own price from a change in the underlying schedule.
4. **Elasticity** — measure responsiveness and examine the relationship between price, quantity sold and revenue.
5. **Price Controls and the Signal** — distinguish maximum and minimum rules, check the price actually used and interpret price information with its limits.
6. **[Audit Who Trades and Who Gains](/supply-and-demand/06-audit-a-market-allocation)** — construct schedules from individual records, separate willingness from completed allocations, and account for payments and gains.
