A café hosts a repair session and collects two sign-up sheets: people who want a spare cable and people who might sell one. To confirm trades, you need a record of who handed over a cable and who paid.

This workshop starts with the whole market record. You will check who is willing at a price, specify who actually trades, and reconstruct the gains without counting the same credit twice.

## Read the record before drawing a curve

<MCQ
  client:visible
  question={"What does a buyer’s maximum acceptable payment tell you?"}
  options={[{"text": "The highest price they are willing and able to pay", "correct": true}, {"text": "The exact price they must pay whenever a trade occurs"}, {"text": "The completed payment already recorded by the café"}]}
  explanation={"A maximum is a threshold for willingness, not a bill or evidence that a transaction happened."}
  pretest={true}
/>

There are five buyers and five sellers in one café session. Each seller owns one spare USB cable, and each buyer wants **at most one cable**. All cables are identical. We use invented **credits**, abbreviated **cr**, as the exchange unit. Each buyer has at least 14 cr available, enough for any listed maximum. The records are constructed inputs, not survey findings.

A buyer's **reservation value** is the most they are willing and able to pay for the cable. A seller's **reservation cost** is the smallest receipt they would accept to give it up. That minimum includes **opportunity cost**: what keeping the cable or using their resources elsewhere is worth to them.

| Buyer | Maximum payment in cr | Seller | Minimum receipt in cr |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| A | 14 | U | 3 |
| B | 12 | V | 5 |
| C | 10 | W | 7 |
| D | 8 | X | 9 |
| E | 6 | Y | 11 |

A’s maximum is 14 cr. That is not A's bill: at a posted price of 8.5 cr, A is willing to buy and would pay 8.5 cr if assigned a completed trade. U's minimum is 3 cr, so U is willing to sell at 8.5 cr. Y has a cable too, but will not offer it at that price because Y requires 11 cr.

For this model, the values are truthful and fixed during the session. Everyone is available, and any willing buyer can trade with any willing seller because the cables are identical. All trades use **one common posted price**. There are no fees, waiting costs, quality changes, outside effects or strategic reports. These assumptions let us focus on the record; a real market audit must check them.

## Turn willingness into a schedule

<MCQ
  client:visible
  question={"The willing counts match. What has the audit established so far?"}
  options={[{"text": "A complete matching is feasible under the stated assumptions", "correct": true}, {"text": "All willing participants have already exchanged cables and credits"}, {"text": "The organiser has selected a unique price and allocation rule"}]}
  explanation={"Counts establish compatibility of plans under the model. Actual participant selection and execution still need to be specified or observed."}
  pretest={true}

At **8.5 cr per cable**, compare every record with that price. Buyers A, B and C have maxima above it. D and E do not. Sellers U, V and W have minima below it. X and Y do not. So **quantity demanded is 3 cables per session**, and **quantity supplied is 3 cables per session**.

A **schedule** collects these counts at different prices. In this finite market, the counts change in steps when a price passes a participant's reservation threshold. They do not have to change after every small price move.

| Posted price in cr per cable | Willing buyers | Willing sellers | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied | Maximum feasible pairs |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 6.5 | A, B, C, D | U, V | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| 8.5 | A, B, C | U, V, W | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 9.5 | A, B, C | U, V, W, X | 3 | 4 | 3 |

Every trade needs one willing buyer and one willing seller. It follows that the maximum possible matching size at a price is the **smaller of the two counts**, under our availability and compatibility assumptions. At 6.5 cr, even four willing buyers cannot make two willing sellers provide four cables.

That count is a bound, not a sales record. An **allocation** names the people who receive the available trading opportunities and how they are paired. **Execution** means the cable and payment actually change hands. To work the initial case, stipulate that **A–U, B–V and C–W all complete their trades at 8.5 cr**. This specific record establishes three completed trades.

The price 8.5 cr clears the listed willingness schedules: the two planned quantities agree. It is not a uniquely required price. Any price strictly between **8 and 9 cr** leaves the same three people willing on each side. Our comparison prices avoid equality with reservation numbers; at equality a participant could be indifferent, which would need an explicit convention.

Use this audit when a report confuses requests, offers and sales. A delivery or transaction record is needed to move from “could match” to “did trade”.

## Account for transfers and gains separately

<MCQ
  client:visible
  question={"Which calculation avoids counting the same payment twice?"}
  options={[{"text": "Add the buyer’s gain and seller’s gain after their deductions", "correct": true}, {"text": "Add the payment to both the buyer’s gain and seller’s receipt"}, {"text": "Count all seller receipts as gains without deducting their costs"}]}
  explanation={"Inside this boundary, a payment leaves one participant and reaches another. The net gain comes from the value of the cable exceeding its opportunity cost."}
  pretest={true}
/>

For a completed trade, the buyer's **gain** is reservation value minus price. The seller's gain is price minus reservation cost. We measure gains relative to keeping the outside option: buyers retain their credits; sellers retain their cables. These are gains on the declared credit scale, not a claim that cash income and well-being are identical.

Work A–U first. A gains **14 − 8.5 = 5.5 cr**. U gains **8.5 − 3 = 5.5 cr**. Together they gain **11 cr**, exactly **14 − 3**. The payment subtracts from A and adds to U, so it cancels when both sides are inside the account.

Here is the complete initial ledger. Every numeric column is in cr; each row is one completed cable trade.

| Trade | Buyer value | Seller cost | Transfer | Buyer gain | Seller gain | Combined gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A–U | 14 | 3 | 8.5 | 5.5 | 5.5 | 11 |
| B–V | 12 | 5 | 8.5 | 3.5 | 3.5 | 7 |
| C–W | 10 | 7 | 8.5 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 3 |
| Total | 36 | 15 | 25.5 | 10.5 | 10.5 | 21 |

Buyer payments equal seller receipts: **3 × 8.5 = 25.5 cr**. That transfer is not an extra 25.5 cr of total gain. The two sides' gains add to **10.5 + 10.5 = 21 cr**, or directly **36 − 15 = 21 cr**.

If the same participants completed trades at another common price between 8 and 9 cr, changing the price would redistribute gains between buyers and sellers. Their combined gain would remain 21 cr because the selected values and costs would be unchanged. Fees, waiting time or outside effects would need additional terms; we excluded them rather than pretending they are always zero.

## Compare two allocations at the same lower price

The café now introduces a rule: nobody may charge **above 6.5 cr per cable**. This maximum allows lower prices too. Separately, the café **chooses and enforces a posted price of exactly 6.5 cr** for this version of the session. The rule alone would not establish that exact price.

The original records give four willing buyers, A/B/C/D, and two willing sellers, U/V. The difference is **4 − 2 = 2 cables of excess demand** at this price. If both offered units trade, two willing buyers remain unserved. The other sellers' physical cables have not vanished; their holders are unwilling to supply them at this price.

Specify two rules and complete their trades. **Rule P** selects A and B; **Rule Q** selects C and D. Both use U and V. Every participating person has a positive gain, and both rules complete two units at the same price.

The same two completed units and the same **13 cr** of payments produce total gains of **18 cr under P** and **10 cr under Q**. Against the initial completed allocation's 21 cr, the differences are **3 cr** and **11 cr**. The selection rule matters as well as quantity.

This metric adds one credit of gain with the same weight whoever receives it. P has a higher total than Q by this measure. The records do not tell us who has greater need, who waited longer or which rule the café regards as fair. Those questions require an objective and evidence beyond the total. Nor does the earlier count of three trades claim a universal maximum under every possible pricing mechanism: our worked market uses a common posted price.

When auditing a constrained market, request the completed participant IDs and allocation rule as well as price and quantity. Without them you cannot reconstruct this distribution of gains.

## Try a changed buyer value

Keep every other assumption and reservation number fixed in a new version of the session. Buyer **E now values a cable at 13 cr rather than 6 cr** because of a new use. E's budget was already sufficient; ability to pay, seller costs, stock and quality have not changed.

Before opening the account below, work out the willing sets at the old posted price of **8.5 cr** and at a proposed new posted price of **9.5 cr**. Then ask which change rewrote the schedule and which only selected a different row of it.
