### Archive case reference

The archive questions use invented teaching data. One clerk has **120 minutes**, with up to **24 routine records** taking **5 minutes each** and **8 complex records** taking **15 minutes each**. Records are indivisible, there is no overtime and all completed records pass an independent acceptance check. Categories come from original intake IDs. The service goal is **at least four complex records, then the largest total number of accepted records**.

The **flat rule** pays one credit per accepted record. The **bounded rule** pays four credits for each of the first four complex records, then one credit for each additional complex record; routine records earn one credit. Assume a clerk maximizes credits. A question that changes a condition says so explicitly.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
An **incentive** changes the payoff of an action. Rewards can involve money, status, penalties or the satisfaction of doing the work itself. Inspect what an arrangement rewards before explaining behaviour through character alone. Incentives can also influence which explanations people find convincing.

A **proxy** is a measurable stand-in for a goal. A higher proxy score need not mean a better result. Check alternative ways to earn the reward, including honest task selection and manipulated reporting. Money can also alter intrinsic or social motives; its effect depends on the setting.

For a proposed rule, state the objective, available actions, time and measurement process. Calculate its best-scoring choices and compare them with the objective. The archive's bounded premium aligns the original model, but a category loophole or changed task time alters that conclusion. Audit quality, displaced work and costs, and evaluate a pilot with a fair comparison. **A calculated optimum describes a model; it does not guarantee a person's behaviour or prove a causal effect.**
</Callout>
