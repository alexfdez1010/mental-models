<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
A circle of competence is useful when it scopes a particular judgment. Familiarity and confidence can prompt an investigation; relevant evidence, conditions and support determine what responsibility is justified. Records, explanations and work samples supply different evidence and retain uncertainty.

Keep all attempts and case-specific help visible. A corrected output can demonstrate a useful supported workflow without proving independent competence. Local critical conditions may matter alongside a total score; a small rehearsal is not a universal qualification.

Compare the actual consequences of independent action, a contained trial, relevant help, delay or refusal. Limited knowledge does not make all losses unlimited, and passing is not automatically costless or bounded. A handoff needs a specific accepted responsibility, inputs, unresolved items, timing and a fallback. Review again when the task or support changes.
</Callout>
