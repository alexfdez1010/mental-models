Alex is good with the club's timetable spreadsheet. A new booking system arrives, and someone says, “You know this stuff. Can you check the bookings and publish the new timetable?” That sentence quietly bundles several jobs into one compliment.

The circle of competence becomes useful when Alex turns the compliment into a claim that can be checked. This workshop follows a complete rehearsal and the handoff it leads to. The people, records, results and local rules are **constructed teaching examples**. They illustrate a decision procedure, not a validated qualification test.

## Specify the claim before testing it

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="A volunteer has handled the old timetable reliably. What should a test for independent use of a new system establish first?"
  options={[
    { text: 'The tasks, conditions and decisions that independent responsibility would cover.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Whether the volunteer feels as confident as they did with the old spreadsheet.' },
    { text: 'Whether another volunteer has called them the club’s technical expert.' }
  ]}
  explanation="Old experience may be useful, but the new claim needs a scope. A test of copying familiar fields is not automatically a test of resolving booking conflicts or authorising publication."
/>

A **competence claim** states what a person can do, under which conditions, and with what support. Alex's initial claim is “I can independently prepare, check and release the timetable.” Split it just enough to see what needs evidence:

| Part of the job | Observable task | What independent responsibility would require |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Copy dates, times and room names without changing their meaning | Correct values and an understood room-name dictionary |
| Check | Detect conflicting or unresolved bookings | Apply the booking rules and leave exceptions unresolved for the responsible person |
| Release | Replace the timetable that members use | Authority to publish and a completed review of the proposed change |

A **rehearsal** is a trial on a copy that cannot change the live timetable. It can reveal a gap without double-booking an actual meeting. It still costs time, and its artificial conditions may leave important parts of the real job untested.

For this rehearsal, Alex may consult the written rules and dictionary: those aids will also be available during the proposed work. A hint about how to answer a particular case counts as **assistance**, because independent work would not include a reviewer supplying that answer. Independence means the declared support conditions, not working with every manual confiscated.

### When to use a scoped claim

Use it when a request crosses from a familiar activity into a different judgment, tool or responsibility. Keep responsibility to publish separate from skill at preparing a file. Someone can perform an activity well and still lack authority to make the final change.

## Check what the existing evidence actually covers

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="Alex presents a folder of successful exports from the old spreadsheet. What is the strongest justified reading?"
  options={[
    { text: 'The files may support familiar export tasks; their coverage and selection still need checking.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Success on the old template proves independent judgment on every new booking rule.' },
    { text: 'Selected successes are worthless and must be treated as evidence of failure.' }
  ]}
  explanation="Useful evidence can be incomplete. Find what tasks the files contain, how they were selected and whether someone else corrected them. Missing failures are not automatically failures, but a success-only folder cannot establish an all-attempt success rate."
/>

Alex's folder contains **18 finished exports**, all from the same old template. Alex selected the files, and the folder has no record of earlier attempts or help. Inspecting their values can support a narrow claim about those files. It does not establish the full attempt count, whether Alex detected unfamiliar exceptions or who made the final corrections. The full attempt count would be the **denominator** of an all-attempt success rate.

Write down the relevant mismatch rather than dismissing the whole person:

| Evidence offered | What it can inform | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Finished old-template exports | Accuracy of the fields that can be inspected | Omitted attempts, corrections and new-template conditions |
| Alex explains the overlap rule | Understanding of that rule in the explanation | Applying it correctly while handling actual records |
| A reviewer repairs an error | What the supported workflow can produce | Whether Alex would detect and handle it without that prompt |

Work samples ask people to perform activities resembling the work to be assessed. The [U.S. Office of Personnel Management describes observing behaviour and task outcomes under relevant work conditions](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/work-samples-and-simulations/). Here, we borrow that principle for a small local rehearsal; we are not adopting a validated employment assessment.

Confidence is another kind of information. It tells you how sure Alex feels. It does not replace evidence about the task. [Kahneman and Klein's analysis of intuitive expertise](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/kahneman-klein-2009.pdf) emphasises relevant environmental cues and opportunities to learn them; it also describes how expertise can differ between tasks within a profession. A confident prediction is worth recording and checking, but willingness to make it is not proof of competence.

## Run a complete bounded work sample

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="Before the rehearsal begins, what prevents a high overall score from hiding a consequential missed exception?"
  options={[
    { text: 'Define both ordinary correctness and the exceptions that block independent release.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Choose the pass rule afterwards so it matches the volunteer’s strongest results.' },
    { text: 'Remove exception cases because routine work is the most common kind.' }
  ]}
  explanation="The purpose is a decision about a stated scope. A predeclared critical condition makes the decision reproducible; an average alone may not address the cost of a specific failure."
/>

The booking coordinator, Marta, prepares **12 separate test cases** and a written answer key before Alex attempts them. She keeps the key separate during the attempt. Every case resets to the same baseline, so one test booking never changes another. All times are on the same day:

- **Oak** already has a confirmed booking from **10:00 to 11:00**
- **Pine** is free throughout **08:00 to 12:00**
- An alias is an alternative room code. The dictionary says **O means Oak** and **P means Pine**; a question mark means the room is unknown
- Meetings may meet at an endpoint: one ending at **10:00** does not overlap one starting at **10:00**
- “Ready” requires the exact room and times and no overlap with the confirmed booking
- A conflict or unknown room requires **hold**, with the specific reason recorded; guessing or publishing is not an acceptable resolution
- Held requests stay out of the published timetable until resolved

A **rubric** is the written scoring rule. A correct case has both the exact prepared fields and the right ready/hold decision, with a reason when on hold. Holding every case would fail the ready cases. A **critical miss** here is leaving a conflicting booking ready for release.

The local rule says a candidate for independent release must handle every case correctly without case-specific help and have no critical miss. Passing would only satisfy this rehearsal gate; it would not establish universal competence or replace the checks required on later live work. Any critical miss means no independent live release now. These are the club's declared teaching-case rules, not a universal pass mark.

| R01 | Oak | 08:00–09:00 | Oak, ready | Oak, ready | Yes |
| R02 | Oak | 09:00–10:00 | Oak, ready | Oak, ready | Yes |

The times are copied exactly in all completed rows. In **C03**, Alex uses the wrong room. In **X02**, the requested interval overlaps the confirmed booking from **10:00 to 10:30**, so leaving it ready is the critical miss. **X03** still lacks a decision when the first attempt is scored; later, Marta prompts Alex to flag the unknown room.

| Routine | 6 | 6 | 6 ÷ 6 = 100% |

## Choose the supported next action

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="The rehearsal contains a missed conflict. What follows under the declared local rule?"
  options={[
    { text: 'Do not grant independent live release now; choose a narrower supported action.', correct: true },
    { text: 'Call the volunteer incompetent at every part of the timetable task.' },
    { text: 'Ignore the miss because most cases had correct results.' }
  ]}
  explanation="The missed conflict matters to the proposed responsibility. It does not erase correct routine work or establish a permanent limit on learning."
/>

Alex does not meet the rehearsal gate for independent release. A useful next action is still available: prepare familiar fields on a copy, with Marta reviewing aliases, exceptions and the complete output before publication. Alex does not receive publication rights as a side effect of passing the routine cases.

| Option now | What it changes | Remaining consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Publish independently now | Removes the reviewer despite the failed gate | A missed conflict could reach the live timetable |
| Prepare on a copy and hand off review | Keeps Alex's useful preparation and separates release authority | Costs review time; the reviewer must receive and accept the work |
| Keep the existing timetable until review is available | Avoids an unreviewed replacement | Delays the update; members may have to wait for new information |

The model does not say that every unfamiliar action has unlimited loss or that delay is free. Here, a copy without publication rights bounds the rehearsal's direct effect on live bookings. The live change has different consequences. Compare the actual options, including what happens if nobody accepts the handoff.

### When to reopen the assessment

Use fresh cases when the purpose is to assess progress after practice. Retain the old attempts as training history. Recheck the claim when the tool, booking rules, case types or available support change. A positive result under one set of aids and conditions does not automatically cover another.

## Close the handoff before relying on it

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="Alex sends a file to a busy coordinator. When may the plan rely on that coordinator’s review?"
  options={[
    { text: 'When the coordinator accepts the specific review responsibility and its timing.', correct: true },
    { text: 'As soon as the file leaves the sender’s outbox.' },
    { text: 'Whenever the sender writes “please check” in the file name.' }
  ]}
  explanation="A sent file is not an accepted responsibility. The handoff needs an agreed task, enough evidence to perform it, a time and an explicit fallback if it cannot be accepted."
/>

The handoff is a small working agreement. Alex sends the **original attempt**, the draft revised after the X03 prompt as a separate version, the source records, dictionary, rubric and unresolved items. Marta's relevant experience is applying these booking rules and checking releases; a general “technical person” label would not identify that expertise.

| Handoff field | Completed entry for this case |
|---|---|
| Requested decision | Review exact values, aliases and all hold/ready decisions; decide whether publication is authorised |
| Known gaps | Wrong room in C03; missed overlap in X02; X03 marked on hold only after a prompt; C03 and X02 still require correction |
| Current state | Copy only; no live booking or publication changes |
| Open item | X03 needs the requester to identify the room; do not invent it |
| Responsible receiver | Marta accepts review of the complete draft by Thursday at 15:30 |
| Return path | Marta records decisions and corrections against IDs; Alex confirms which version was reviewed |
| Fallback | If review is not accepted or cannot finish in time, keep the existing timetable and tell members the update is delayed |

Acceptance of review does not mean approval to publish. Marta still needs to complete the check and resolve or retain the holds under the club's rule. A specialist is also fallible: unclear reasoning, a new rule or unresolved disagreement calls for clarification and, when needed, another relevant review.

## Transfer the procedure to a different task

<MCQ client:visible pretest
  question="A subtitle editor flags an unclear name after a reviewer points out the guess. What should the assessment record?"
  options={[

    { text: 'Only the corrected result, because prompts are part of every independent assessment.' },
    { text: 'A permanent inability to edit any subtitles in the future.' }
  ]}
  explanation="The distinction between a useful supported result and independent performance transfers to another kind of work. The support conditions still need to match the claim."
/>

A club now asks Sam to prepare subtitles. The proposed task includes both timing the captions and handling unclear names. The key for this constructed rehearsal requires exact words on clear clips and **an explicit unclear-name flag rather than a guess** on ambiguous clips. All six clips are included:

| Clips | Required outcome | First attempt | Correct independently |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01–S04 | Accurate timed captions for clear speech | All accurate, without prompts | 4 |
| S05 | Flag the unclear name and preserve the time | Correct flag, without a prompt | 1 |
| S06 | Flag the unclear name and preserve the time | An invented name, timing preserved | 0 |

A reviewer points out the guessed name in **S06**, and Sam replaces it with the required unclear-name flag. Before reading on, decide: how many results were independently correct, what does the correction add, and which responsibility should stay with a reviewer now?

<details>
<summary>Check the transfer decision</summary>

The first attempt has **4 + 1 = 5 correct out of 6**. After the prompted flag, the output has **6 correct out of 6**, including an assisted correction. Timing alone does not cover name verification. Keep the first attempt and help visible; do not call the corrected set a fresh independent demonstration.

Under a local rule against publishing guessed names, retain review of unclear names before publication. Sam can still contribute timing and a draft. Future independent responsibility needs relevant fresh evidence under declared support conditions; repeating these same answers would be practice. The example does not estimate a long-run error rate from six clips.
