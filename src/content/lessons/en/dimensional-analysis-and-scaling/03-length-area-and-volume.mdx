Changing a unit preserves an object. This lesson changes the object itself. We will compare **geometrically similar** closed cubes: every length scales by the same positive factor, so the shape stays the same.

Picture one coat of paint on a cube and the material filling its interior. Paint covers faces; the interior fills space. Enlarging every edge changes those two quantities by different factors.

## Separate length, area and volume

For a closed cube with positive edge $a$, one face has area $a^2$, all six faces have total area $A=6a^2$, and its volume is $V=a^3$. If the edge is multiplied by $k>0$,

$$
a'=ka,\qquad A'=k^2A,\qquad V'=k^3V.
$$

For ten and twenty centimetre edges, the area is six hundred and twenty-four hundred square centimetres. Volume rises from one thousand to eight thousand cubic centimetres. Halving an edge gives one quarter of the area and one eighth of the volume. These are geometric relations under the stated shape and exposure conditions.

### When the geometric power is useful

Use it for a shape-preserving geometric comparison after deciding which quantity matters. The model does not make total cost, strength or cooling rate automatically follow one of these powers. Such outcomes can depend on additional processes and parameters.

## Compare a surface with its enclosed volume

Divide the cube formulas:

$$
\frac{A}{V}=\frac{6a^2}{a^3}=\frac6a.
$$

Doubling the edge halves this ratio. It carries inverse-length units: $0.6\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ for the ten-centimetre cube and $0.3\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ for the twenty-centimetre cube. Expressing the original cube’s edge as 0.1 metre gives $60\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, the same physical ratio in another unit. A changing numerical representation is separate from a changed cube.

There is a second comparison in the explorer. Eight separate cubes of ten-centimetre edge have total volume $8\times1000=8000$ cubic centimetres, equal to one twenty-centimetre cube. Their total exposed area is $8\times600=4800$ square centimetres, twice the large cube’s twenty-four hundred. Holding total volume fixed does not hold exposed surface fixed.

### When an exposed-surface argument needs a boundary

Specify whether faces touch, are covered or remain exposed. Also specify whether you compare one object or a group. A diagram of one representative cube needs the number in its group beside it; otherwise a same-volume comparison can look like a same-count comparison.

## Change the contact condition and recount

Now join the eight small cubes into a two-by-two-by-two solid block. Each contact hides two small faces from the exterior. There are four contacts along each of the three directions: twelve contacts hide twenty-four faces. The separate group had forty-eight exposed small faces; twenty-four remain exposed after joining.

Each small face has area one hundred square centimetres. The joined block therefore exposes $24\times100=2400$ square centimetres. Its volume remains eight thousand cubic centimetres. You changed the exposure assumption, so the separate-cube area total no longer applies.

### When to recount instead of extrapolating

Recount after changing contacts, openings or the definition of “surface”. The same habit applies to a collection whose parts become connected: identify what the new boundary includes. The explorer deliberately retains separate closed cubes; it does not switch to the joined-block model behind the controls.

## Reconstruct a mixed cost instead of choosing one power

Consider a constructed model, with credits abbreviated cr:

$$
K=10\,\mathrm{cr}+\left(3\,\mathrm{cr/m^2}\right)A+\left(2\,\mathrm{cr/m^3}\right)V.
$$

The coefficients have units that make every term a cost. For a one-metre cube, the area term is eighteen credits and the volume term two credits, so the total is thirty. For a two-metre cube, the terms are seventy-two and sixteen, plus the same fixed ten: ninety-eight credits.

An area-only extrapolation would multiply the original **whole total** by four and predict one hundred twenty credits. A volume-only extrapolation would multiply it by eight and predict two hundred forty. Both mishandle terms with a different scaling rule.

### When a single power is justified

Check which terms are present. If a model contains only a fixed coefficient times area, geometrically similar shapes produce an area power. The declared mixed model contains additional nonzero terms. If its coefficients, material or production method change, recompute rather than carrying the old total forward.
