This is the final exam for **Bottlenecks & the Theory of Constraints**. It covers serial capacity, the five focusing steps, local and system gains, Amdahl’s law, moving constraints and the evidence needed to choose an upgrade. Read the objective and assumptions in each case before comparing the alternatives.

For the simple serial-line rate calculations, assume dedicated resources, one visit to each stage, stable effective capacities, sufficient materials and buffers, unchanged quality and no rework. Treat the line as already operating. Where the question asks for throughput at capacity, enough work is available to use that capacity. These assumptions do not turn a queue snapshot into proof or make a capacity estimate an observed completion count.

### Calibration-upgrade case

The following **hypothetical** case is used by one applied question. It has the serial-line assumptions above, no older backlog and no inventory-building target. The objective is to serve confirmed new orders, with unchanged quality.

| Quantity | Before the upgrade | After the proposed upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Intake capacity in kits/day | 48 | 48 |
| Calibration capacity in kits/day | 20 | 35 |
| Verification capacity in kits/day | 30 | 30 |
| Packing capacity in kits/day | 40 | 40 |
| Confirmed new demand in kits/day | 26 | 26 |

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
For a simple serial line, compare compatible effective stage capacities to find its internal ceiling. Then check demand, older backlog and the operating assumptions before treating that ceiling as an output plan. Queue histories help diagnose the cause when combined with matched arrival, service and availability records. Use the focusing steps to compare recoverable losses and expansion, preserving quality and safety. After every change, recalculate: the next stage or demand may limit the gain. Amdahl’s law separately quantifies how unchanged portions of a task limit its time reduction. Commit to an upgrade only when the estimated system gain serves the objective and survives a representative trial.
</Callout>
