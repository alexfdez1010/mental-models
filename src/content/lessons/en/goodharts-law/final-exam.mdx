<summary>Workshop model reference</summary>

These are invented probability catalogues, not promised sample frequencies. Each original type has probability 1/4. Genuine validity G and original type are hidden from the organiser, who sees only the final score. The objective is correct classification with equal weight for false acceptance and false rejection.

| Type | G | Original score x |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0 | 0 |
| B | 0 | 1 |
| C | 1 | 2 |
| D | 1 | 3 |

Accept at a final score at least the cutoff. Rule cutoffs are Open 0, Low 1, Original 2, Raised 3, High 4 and Top 5. Participants maximise **2.5 × accepted − k × e**, with accepted equal to 0 or 1, integer edits **e = 0…4**, final score **x + e** without a clamp, and unchanged G. The initial cost is **k = 1**, transfer cost **k = 2** and costly-edit counterexample **k = 3**. No other motives or actions enter. For the diagnostic comparison, the alternative passive protocol permits no edits and has population weights A = 0, B = 0, C = 3/4, D = 1/4. Only final scores and acceptance are matched across those two histories, not validity or accuracy.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
A proxy can change its usefulness when it is targeted. Selection, changed regimes, causal intervention and strategic response need different diagnoses; incentives, hidden information and feedback describe one important combination. Compare the actual goal with the score and test each safeguard against its stated mechanism and cost. The workshop shows both a failed rule and a targeted rule that remains accurate under a different editing cost. Equal proxy observations alone do not establish equal outcomes or identify gaming.
</Callout>
