Doubling a file can cost very little. Doubling its information is a different project.

## Define the independent unit

  pretest={true}
  question={"Two independent fair bits are each copied four times. The file contains eight rows. Which input determines the variability of its mean?"}
  options={[{"text":"Eight independent fair bits.","correct":false},{"text":"Two independent fair bits with equal total weights.","correct":true},{"text":"One independent fair bit with eight different values.","correct":false}]}
  explanation={"The mean of the eight rows is the mean of the two underlying bits. The copies add weight but no new randomness."}

A **Bernoulli trial** is a binary observation with a specified probability of 1. In the first model, eight trials are independent and each has probability 1/2. Independence means knowing the other outcomes does not change a trial's probability; the joint law factors into the product of its component probabilities. Identically distributed means the same probability law is used for each trial.

In the second model, generate independent fair bits $Z_1,Z_2$. Write four copies of $Z_1$ and four of $Z_2$. The eight-row mean is $(4Z_1+4Z_2)/8=(Z_1+Z_2)/2$. Its possible values are 0, 1/2 and 1 with probabilities 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4. Its variance is $1/8$. The independent eight-row mean instead has variance $(1/4)/8=1/32$.

Even the probability of an extreme file differs. Eight independent fair bits are all equal with probability $2/2^8=1/128$. The copied file is all equal when its two underlying bits match, with probability $2/4=1/2$. A catalogued probability is not an empirical frequency measured from a supplied dataset.

## Design effects are comparisons tied to an estimator

<MCQ client:visible
  className="not-prose"
  pretest={true}
  question={"The copied mean has four times the variance of the independent eight-row mean. What does an effective sample size of two mean here?"}
  options={[{"text":"Every analysis of the file must behave as if it contained two rows.","correct":false},{"text":"The copied mean equals the independent eight-row mean in every file.","correct":false},{"text":"An independent mean with two fair trials has the same variance.","correct":true}]}
  explanation={"Effective sample size matches a chosen variance to a specified independent reference. It is not an intrinsic count valid for every statistic."}
/>

The **design effect** is the variance under the actual design divided by the variance under a stated reference design, for the same target and estimator comparison. Here $\operatorname{DEFF}=(1/8)/(1/32)=4$. Matching the variance to an independent fair-bit mean gives **effective sample size** $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=8/4=2$.

Dependence does not always increase variance. Lesson two's sample of two distinct cards from eight has variance $3/28$, compared with $1/8$ for two independent fair bits. That comparison gives design effect $6/7$ and effective sample size $2/(6/7)=7/3$. A noninteger effective size larger than two is coherent: it is a variance equivalence, not a hidden third observed card.

### When to use the comparison

State the estimator, target, independent reference and actual design before reporting a design effect. Group similarity, unequal weights and sampling without replacement can have different effects. A single “effective n” does not settle frame bias, measurement validity, tail probabilities or precision for every other statistic. The [CDC’s variance tutorial](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/tutorials/varianceestimation.aspx) explains why clustering must be included in design-based precision calculations; the exact numbers here come from our toy laws.

## Spend a collection budget on a defined menu

Suppose two existing independent fair bits each already contribute four rows. The report must use the equally weighted mean of **all** rows collected. You may spend at most four fictional collection units. A copied row is not claimed to be a new person, trial or measurement.

| Plan | Independent-bit weights in the final mean | Final rows | Cost | Sampling variance |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Retain existing file | 4, 4 | 8 | 0 | 1/8 |
| Copy every existing row once | 8, 8 | 16 | 1 | 1/8 |
| Add two new groups of four identical rows | 4, 4, 4, 4 | 16 | 4 | 1/16 |
| Add four new individual rows | 4, 4, 1, 1, 1, 1 | 12 | 4 | 1/16 |

Every newly introduced bit is independent of the old bits and other new bits, with probability 1/2. Group collection costs two units per new bit; individual collection costs one. These are stipulated costs and the four rows labelled “plan” are the entire allowed menu.

For independent fair bits with weights $w_i$, the weighted mean has variance

$$\frac{\sum_i w_i^2}{4(\sum_i w_i)^2}.$$

This follows by expanding the squared weighted sum: covariance terms vanish by independence and each bit has variance 1/4. The last plan has squared weights summing to $16+16+1+1+1+1=36$, so variance is $36/(4\times12^2)=1/16$. The grouped plan gives $64/(4\times16^2)=1/16$ too.

### Transfer and decision

The decision criterion is cost at most four and variance at most 1/16 for the stipulated all-row mean. Which plans qualify? Is there a uniquely preferred plan from those criteria alone?

<details>
<summary>Apply the two constraints</summary>

Adding two new groups and adding four individuals both cost four and reach variance 1/16. Retaining or copying the file leaves variance 1/8 and fails the precision requirement. The two qualifying plans tie on the stated criteria; a unique choice needs another objective, such as permitted collection format. We have not searched every conceivable estimator or collection design, so this is not a global optimality theorem.

</details>
