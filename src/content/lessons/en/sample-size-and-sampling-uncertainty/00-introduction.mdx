## Eight rows, two different experiments

A **binary observation** has two values: marked is 1 and unmarked is 0. The **sample proportion** is the number marked divided by the number of rows. In one constructed experiment, each of eight independent fair bits has probability 1/2 of being marked. In another, we generate only two independent fair bits, then copy each into four rows. These are alternative probability models, not two records of the same realised experiment.

Both proportions have expectation 1/2. Their **sampling variances**, the average squared departures from that expectation over the stipulated repeated experiment, are 1/32 and 1/8. Copies quadruple the variance relative to the independent-row reference here. We will derive those fractions in lesson four.

In the copied file, marked counts of 1, 2 or 3 cannot occur. They remain in the full table with zero probability, but cannot be selected as an observed count. Its only counts are 0, 4 and 8. An observed proportion of 1/2 is compatible with both models; that one agreement does not identify the data-collection process.

### When to use this audit

Use it when a claim becomes stronger merely because a dataset became larger. Ask whether the added records represent new units, repeated measurements, copies or a changed frame. A model can quantify the uncertainty it defines; it cannot make omitted populations or measurement errors disappear.

### A first transfer

A colleague adds a second copy of every row and says the uncertainty must fall. What would you request before accepting that claim?

<details>
<summary>Compare your audit request</summary>

Request the estimator, the dependence between old and new rows, and the rule generating genuinely new observations. Exact duplicates leave the original sample proportion unchanged for every possible original file. Its sampling distribution and standard error therefore remain unchanged. This conclusion is about that statistic under that duplication rule, not every possible use of repeated measurements.

</details>
