Two teams report enough benefit to approve a shared installation. The reports clear the threshold, but that alone tells you neither whether the reports are true nor who funds the bill. We will audit one small rule completely: messages in, allocation and payments out, then each team’s opportunity to change its message.

<Callout variant="info" title="A constructed finite model">
All credits, values and budgets below are invented. They define utilities and accounts, not a measured market or a prediction of reporting behavior. We test the stated message space and unilateral deviations; coalitions and side payments are excluded.
</Callout>

## Fix the types and the protocol

Each of two exhibition teams, A and B, privately values one shared installation at **0, 3 or 7 credits**. Credits, abbreviated **cr**, are a fictional transferable utility unit. A **type** is that true private value. A **report** is the message sent to the organizer; sending another number does not change the value.

The organizer commits to a deterministic rule and its financing before simultaneous sealed messages. Each team can report **exit, 0, 3 or 7**. If either exits, the project is cancelled. In any cancellation there is no construction, resource cost, payment or entry fee. The outside option gives each team utility zero. If built, the project costs **8 cr once**, and both teams receive their respective true benefit.

A team’s **quasilinear utility** is its true benefit minus its payment when built, and zero when cancelled. Each team can afford any payment the rules charge, at most 5 cr. There are no message costs, side payments, interdependent values or collusion. The organizer is a committed financing source, not a third player whose participation we prove. These conditions are part of the model.

### When to use it

Write this specification before calling a mechanism truthful or voluntary. An unlisted message, a different outside option or a new payment obligation changes the question.

## Find a profitable deviation

Begin with **equal share**. Build if both reports are numeric and their sum is at least 8; charge each team 4. Otherwise cancel and charge zero. The threshold is inclusive, although the possible numeric sums in this domain never equal 8. No external funding is needed.

Fix true values **(3,7)** and hold B’s report at 7. If A tells the truth, reports (3,7) build. A receives benefit 3 and pays 4: utility **−1**. Reporting zero or exiting cancels: utility **0**. Either deviation gains **1 cr**. Reporting seven still builds and charges four, so its utility remains −1.

### When to use it

A counterexample needs a fixed type, fixed other report, a permitted alternative and its utility gain. A complaint about unfairness alone is not that calculation.

## Test every other report

Now use **critical payment**. Keep the same allocation threshold. When built, A pays **8 minus B’s report**, and B pays **8 minus A’s report**. All other numeric reports are below the cost, so each team is critical whenever construction occurs. Cancelled projects still charge zero. The organizer commits up to 6 cr to cover the remaining cost.

At reports (3,7), A pays 1 and B pays 5. With true values (3,7), their utilities are **2 and 2**. For A of true value 3 facing report 7, exit and zero give utility 0, while reports three and seven both give utility 2. A false report can tie truth.

The reason is checkable without assuming B tells the truth. If B reports exit or zero, A cannot trigger construction with any allowed message, so all utilities are zero. If B reports 3, building charges A 5: type 7 prefers to build; types 0 and 3 prefer cancellation. If B reports 7, building charges A 1: types 3 and 7 prefer to build; type 0 prefers cancellation. In each case the truthful report makes the preferred allocation and pays no more than its true benefit. The same argument applies to B by symmetry.

## Reconcile the funding and the objective

At truthful values (3,7), the critical payments total **1+5=6**, leaving **2** for the organizer. At reports (7,7), payments total **1+1=2**, leaving **6**. The unchanged eight-credit project can therefore need different outside funding depending on reports. The payment rule supplies truthful incentives by giving up budget balance among the two teams.

Define **welfare** here as the two true benefits minus the resource cost: **W=(vA+vB−8)** if built, and zero if cancelled. Payments finance that one cost; subtracting all payments and then subtracting the cost again would double count. The accounts reconcile as **uA+uB−organizer contribution=W**. With true values (7,7), critical payments give utilities 6 and 6; subtract organizer 6 to obtain welfare 6, equal to 14−8.

Under truthful reports, the critical rule builds exactly when the true sum is at least 8, so it maximizes this welfare between the two declared alternatives for all nine type pairs. Each team’s truthful utility is nonnegative for every other allowed report, satisfying the stated **individual-rationality (IR)** inequality against zero. That claim concerns the teams. The organizer pays a subsidy and has no modeled benefit or participation proof. Equality with the outside option also does not force someone to join.

List who is inside the welfare account and whose participation is being claimed. Funding a project and maximizing a stated welfare function are separate tests.

Ask whether a claim requires one desired equilibrium or all equilibria to work. An incentive proof by itself does not supply an equilibrium-selection rule.

## Reaudit a changed funding rule

The organizer’s maximum contribution falls from 6 to **2 cr**. A proposed patch retains the old critical payments but cancels whenever they leave more than 2 to fund. The cap is inclusive: a need of 2 is allowed. This changes allocation as well as funding. All payments and resource costs remain zero on cancellation.

Use new true values **(7,7)** and hold B’s report at 7. Compare A reporting 7 with A reporting 3. Calculate construction, A’s payment and utility in each case before opening the solution.

There is a sharp limit to transporting the repair. Explicitly enlarge both the true-value set and numeric messages to **0, 2, 3, 7**, keeping the same flat-three rule, cost, cap and zero outside option. A type 2 facing report 7 now tells the truth, builds and gets **2−3=−1**; reporting 0 or exiting gives 0. The old finite proof did not cover this added type/message. ### When to use it

Reaudit after changing a cap, fee, participation condition or message set. A counterexample to one patch does not rule out every other mechanism; a successful finite repair does not authorize an unrestricted theorem.
