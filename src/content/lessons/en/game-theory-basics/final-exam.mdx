<summary>Reference rules for the mixed-strategy questions</summary>

The constructed games use simultaneous choices without observing the rival's realised action. Each player maximises expected utility. Entries below are Row's utility points; Column's utility is the negative in every cell. H means Heads and T means Tails.

| Row / Column | Case A: H | Case A: T | Case B: H | Case B: T |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H | $1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $-1$ |
| T | $-1$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $1$ |

In case A's biased **independent** profile, Row chooses H with probability $1/4$ and Column with probability $3/4$. A deviation holds the other player's probability rule fixed. Its gain is best available expected utility minus the current mean.

The **independent half** mechanism uses two private fair bits. The **shared-same** mechanism sends the same fair recommended action to both. For the latter comparison, both know the rule, see their own recommendation before acting and may follow it, flip it or choose a constant action. Following gives only HH or TT, each with probability $1/2$. No shared recommendation is silently treated as an independent draw.

Case B changes only the HH utility pair. Its equilibrium probabilities and utilities must be recomputed.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
Game theory is **second-order thinking aimed at another strategist**: when your
payoff depends on someone else’s choice, write the situation as a **payoff matrix**,
then solve it. Grab a **dominant strategy** if one exists; otherwise find a **Nash
equilibrium** — a pure or mixed strategy profile where neither player improves by changing alone. For a mixed profile, specify the joint law, compute both means and check both deviation gains; shared recommendations require their own information and response rules.
The **prisoner’s dilemma** is the painful case where defection is dominant and both
rational players lose, the hidden engine under arms races, price wars and the tragedy
of the commons — and you escape it not by moralising but by **changing the game**,
above all through **repetition**, where **tit-for-tat** (nice, retaliatory, forgiving,
clear) makes cooperation rational. But not every game is a dilemma: the **stag hunt**
turns on trust, **chicken** on credible nerve, and the costliest error of all is the
**fixed-pie fallacy** — fighting zero-sum over a pie you could have grown. *Always
diagnose the game before you choose your move.*
</Callout>
