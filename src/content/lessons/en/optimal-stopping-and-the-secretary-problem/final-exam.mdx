## Numerical cards for the questions

The **reference card** uses the workshop's six-option classical model: known horizon, perfect relative comparisons only, no ties, no recall or inspection costs, one available selection, every order equally likely, and reward one only for the unique greatest. A cutoff rejects its opening group, selects the first later record, and otherwise selects the final option.

| Opening rejections | Winning orders | All orders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 120 | 720 |
| 1 | 274 | 720 |
| 2 | 308 | 720 |
| 3 | 282 | 720 |
| 4 | 216 | 720 |
| 5 | 120 | 720 |

**Trace card T** is a completed analyst record: $(2,4,5,1,6,3)$ with two opening rejections. Higher completed labels are better. During selection, only comparisons among the revealed prefix are available; the chooser does not see these global labels or the unseen remainder.

**Numerical card V is a different reward model.** There are two opportunities including the current one. Accepting now earns 60 quality points. Rejecting it permanently permits a final numerical payoff of 0 or 100 points, each with probability $1/2$, with an additional inspection cost of 5 points. The final draw must be accepted, there is no recall or outside option, and past costs are excluded. Maximize expected net points, rather than best-choice probability.

The **changed catalogue** replaces only the reference arrival law. It is known before cutoff choice that these are the only possible complete orders, each with probability $1/4$, and the greatest is always last. The particular row and unseen global labels are not revealed during play.

| Permitted order | Complete analyst labels |
| --- | --- |
| A | 5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 |
| B | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 |
| C | 2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 6 |
| D | 1, 3, 2, 5, 4, 6 |

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways — the whole course">
Specify the reward, information, actions and arrival law before choosing a stopping rule. In the classical model, compare finite opening cutoffs and distinguish their sampling fraction from their success probability. A live decision cannot use the completed future. Re-evaluate when availability, costs, objectives or the order law change; exact arithmetic does not verify those assumptions.
</Callout>
