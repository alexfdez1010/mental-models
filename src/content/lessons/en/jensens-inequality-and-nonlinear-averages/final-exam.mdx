The main work response is $c(x)=\alpha x^2$, with coefficient one staff-minute per job squared. For normalized comparison laws, response values are response points. Weights are normalized by their positive total; only positive-weight input values belong to support. All relevant functions in the shape questions are defined on the full stated interval, unless a question identifies a domain failure.

For a finite convex response on an interval containing the support and its weighted means:

$$
f\left(\sum_i p_i x_i\right)\le\sum_i p_i f(x_i).
$$

Strict convexity plus at least two distinct positive-weight inputs makes this strict. Concavity reverses the direction. Variance is $E[X^2]-(E[X])^2$. An explicit conditional replacement with $E[Y\mid X]=X$ gives the all-convex expected-response comparison by applying finite Jensen within branches.

For the conditional replacement question, X is two/four equally. From parent two, Y is one/three equally; from parent four, Y is three/five equally. Thus the four complete paths each have probability one quarter.

The two hinges are $h_2(x)=\max(0,x-2)$ and $h_3(x)=\max(0,x-3)$, convex on the full comparison interval $[0,4]$. The capped response in its separate question is $g(x)=\min(x^2,9)$ on $[0,6]$.

All jobs in each planning case are available at the start. Both plans must complete the same jobs in the listed number of batches, each with capacity six. There is no deadline, arrival restriction or extra per-batch setup charge. Complete work is square processing work plus the stated coordination, in staff-minutes. Four-batch budget is sixty; six-batch budget seventy. The objective is lower complete work among the two listed feasible plans. Equal batch weights describe the complete catalogue; no future sampling law is supplied.
