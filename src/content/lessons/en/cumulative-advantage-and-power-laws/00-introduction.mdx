Two equally visible articles receive different first links. A platform could use existing links to decide which article to show next. In that **constructed rule**, an early link changes later chances. To explain an actual article’s success, we would also need evidence that the platform used such a rule and that other relevant conditions had been considered.

This course follows that distinction: **a rule about how gains are allocated**, **a distribution of possible outcomes**, and **an explanation of observations** are different claims. A large winner does not settle all three.

**Cumulative advantage** is a reinforcing process in which an existing advantage helps obtain further gains. **Preferential attachment** specifies such a connection in an allocation or network model. The **Matthew effect** concerns cumulative recognition in a social setting. These ideas are related; treating their names as interchangeable does not supply all the assumptions of a mathematical model.

Take a small example with no hidden differences in quality. An urn holds one red ball and one blue ball. Choose uniformly among the balls, note the colour, return the ball and add one more of that colour. After a first red draw there are two red balls and one blue. The next red probability is **2/3**, compared with **1/2** if we only returned the drawn ball. Neither number is a guarantee.

### When the model helps

Use a reinforcement model when you can specify what accumulates and how that state changes the next allocation. To use it as an explanation of a real system, investigate that connection rather than infer it from the size of the winner.

## A power law is a separate claim

A **power law** describes a mathematical relationship in which a quantity varies as a power of another, over a stated range. Some growing-network models with specified attachment rules produce power-law tails. A fixed-colour urn is a different model. Its finite count distribution must be calculated from its own rule; it is not automatically a power law.

The fat-tails prerequisite already separates a population mean, an estimate from a sample and a typical observation. Keep those distinctions here. The existence of a mean depends on the distribution and its parameters. A reinforcing process need not make every mean meaningless, and a straight-looking log–log plot does not identify a unique generating mechanism.

<Callout variant="info" title="Name the object before interpreting the number">
A count, a share, a probability and a fitted exponent answer different questions.
</Callout>

In the urn, the number of red observations differs from the number of red balls left inside. In a dataset, an observed concentration differs from evidence that reinforcement caused it.

## The course route

1. **What a power law is** introduces the mathematical shape and the need to distinguish a specified relation from an empirical fit.
2. **The mechanism: preferential attachment** works a finite reinforcing rule and contrasts it with giving every quantity the same percentage growth.
3. **The 80/20 principle** considers shares of items and shares of output. Treat any nested split as an additional assumption to check.
4. **Winner-take-all** considers reward structures and scalability. An unequal reward is not a measurement of a skill gap.
5. **The tyranny of the tail** considers large observations and planning. Check the distribution and the objective before choosing a summary.
6. **Where the model lies** examines identification, selection and the limits of a distributional description.
7. **[Follow a reinforced draw](/cumulative-advantage-and-power-laws/07-follow-a-reinforced-draw)** enumerates three draws, compares complete count laws and changes the starting urn for an independent transfer.
8. **The final exam** is one irreversible run, with a 70% pass mark. Submitted answers remain locked.

The applied workshop supplies explicit rules so you can check a complete example. Elsewhere in the course, ask which claims are assumptions, which are deductions and which would require observations of a real system.
