A model can be stable and still imply a long average delay. In M/M/1, the difference between service and arrival rates controls mean total stay. As that difference narrows, equal changes in a rate need not produce equal changes in time.

We will compare model consequences first. Choosing a real upgrade additionally needs evidence, costs and an objective.

At $\lambda=6$, total stay is $60/(10-6)=15$ minutes. At 8 it is $60/(10-8)=30$. At 9 it is $60/(10-9)=60$. Moving from 6 to 8 doubles mean total stay; moving only one more unit, from 8 to 9, doubles it again.

Those waits do not come from slower mean service. The service parameter remains 10 per hour. The model attributes the changing mean to the arrival/service combination under its assumptions.

### When utilization helps

In this stable M/M/1, load $\rho=\lambda/\mu$ is also the stationary busy fraction. But utilization alone does not fix the clock. Compare $\lambda=4,\mu=5$ with $\lambda=8,\mu=10$: both have $\rho=4/5$ and $L=4$, while total stay is 60 versus 30 minutes. Doubling both rates keeps the relative load but halves the time scale.

Increasing service to 12 per hour and reducing admitted arrivals to 6 per hour both produce a 15-minute mean total stay. They do not produce identical waiting, occupancy or offered work.

For $\lambda=8,\mu=12$, mean service is five minutes and mean waiting is ten. For $\lambda=6,\mu=10$, mean service is six minutes and mean waiting is nine. Both add to fifteen.

The table provides no implementation costs, lost-demand consequences or preference between those outcomes. It therefore identifies two ways to meet one model criterion, rather than a universally best policy.

### When the objective changes

A target for waiting before service is not the same as a target for total stay. A policy that rejects some requests also changes who enters the measured system. Check whose outcome matters before comparing a faster server with fewer admitted jobs.

## Turn a requirement into an inequality

<MCQ
  client:visible
  className={"not-prose"}
  pretest={true}
  question={"The criterion is mean total stay at most 15 min. In the stable domain, which rate gap is sufficient and necessary?"}
  options={[{"text":"μ−λ≥15 jobs/h"},{"text":"μ−λ≤4 jobs/h"},{"text":"μ−λ≥4 jobs/h","correct":true}]}
  explanation={"60/(μ−λ)≤15 gives μ−λ≥4."}
/>


Suppose the declared criterion is **mean total stay at most 15 minutes**, under the same M/M/1 assumptions. Convert it to hours and solve:

$$
\frac1{\mu-\lambda}\le\frac14
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu-\lambda\ge4\ \text{jobs/hour}.
$$

The denominator is positive in the stable domain, which is why this rearrangement is valid. A candidate outside that domain cannot pass by returning a negative number.

For possible demand rates in the interval $[6,8]$ jobs per hour, capacity 10 meets the criterion at 6 but not throughout the interval. Capacity 12 has $\mu-\lambda\ge12-8=4$ everywhere in the interval, so it meets the stated mean criterion across that catalogue.

### When a robust catalogue is useful

Use it to expose which stated cases a candidate passes. To choose between candidates, add their feasible budgets, costs and consequences. Passing this model's criterion does not establish an economically optimal capacity or compliance with a real service standard.

## Keep the guarantee at the level it supports

<MCQ
  client:visible
  className={"not-prose"}
  pretest={true}
  question={"An M/M/1 mean total stay is 15 minutes. What follows about every individual job?"}
  options={[{"text":"None may exceed fifteen."},{"text":"Exactly half take fifteen."},{"text":"No individual upper bound follows from this mean.","correct":true}]}
  explanation={"The exponential service law itself allows longer individual services."}
/>


A mean of fifteen minutes does not cap every job at fifteen. Exponential service durations already allow longer services; those jobs' total stays cannot be shorter than their own service. The mean describes a model average, not an individual deadline guarantee.

The deterministic example from lesson 3 has zero waiting while the server works continuously across its 0–8-minute window. Extending that perfectly synchronized arrival/service pattern keeps waiting zero at relative load one. Its arrival and service rules differ from M/M/1, so it is no contradiction of the stationary model boundary. It does contradict a universal claim that every queue must behave like that one stochastic model.
