<summary>Constructed reference records</summary>

Within each census, A and B are counted at the same declared stage. The original census in Copy, Scaled, Arrival, Mark and Declining is four A parents and twelve B parents. Parents are excluded from the offspring census.

| Record | Locally born A | Locally born B | A arrivals | B arrivals | Transmission |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copy | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | All children retain the parental type |
| Scaled | 24 | 24 | 0 | 0 | All children retain the parental type |
| Arrival | 4 | 12 | 8 | 0 | Local children retain the parental type |
| Mark | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | Each ancestry group has six children of each externally assigned label |
| Declining | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | Children retain the parental type |

Copy stipulates three surviving offspring per A parent and one per B parent. Scaled stipulates six and two. Arrival stipulates one per local parent before the arrivals. In Declining, two A parents and two B parents each supply one child; all other parents supply none.

| Mark ancestry | A-labelled children | B-labelled children |
|---|---|---|
| A parents | 6 | 6 |
| B parents | 6 | 6 |

For the environment card, use the original four A and twelve B parents. The reversed rule gives each A parent one A child and each B parent three B children.

| Survivor card | Original individuals | Listed survivors |
|---|---|---|
| A | 50 | 45 |
| B | 50 | 30 |

| Group-contribution card | Original parents | Surviving offspring |
|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | 40 |
| B | 20 | 60 |

| Two-stage reproduction card | Original parents | Reproducing survivors | Offspring per reproducing survivor |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | 5 | 4 |
| B | 10 | 10 | 3 |

These are separate constructed records, not estimates about a species or a single shared population.

## Course recap

<MindMap eyebrow="" client:visible className="not-prose" title="Reconstruct the model and its limits" root={{"label":"A selection explanation to audit","children":[{"label":"Define types and census stages"},{"label":"Count reproductive contributions and transmission"},{"label":"Separate observed identities from supported predictions"},{"label":"Check arrivals, sampling and changed labels"},{"label":"Distinguish relative share, absolute growth, cause and value"}]}} />

A contribution calculation needs a defined population and interval. A selection explanation also needs evidence for the proposed mechanism. The same final share can result from different histories, and a greater share can accompany fewer individuals. Apply a copying model with its assumptions visible; describing an outcome does not assign it a moral value.
