## Reference cards for distinct models

**Curved prototype for questions five and six:** use $v(x)=x^{\alpha}$ for $x\ge0$ and $v(x)=-\lambda(-x)^{\alpha}$ for $x<0$, with $\alpha=22/25$ and $\lambda=9/4$. Use one period with 150 final credits certain versus 100 or 200, each with probability one half; compare references 100 and 200. These questions concern that menu under the declared curve and ordinary probability weights. The multiplier compares matched nonzero score magnitudes; it is not a ratio of finite derivatives at zero. The linear workshop has no within-arm curvature, and the cumulative account can use decision weights different from physical probabilities.

**Historical framing item eight:** the [original 1981 questionnaire](https://stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/Tversky-Kahneman-1981.pdf) compared separate respondent groups. It recorded $72\%$ choosing the certain program in one frame and $78\%$ choosing the risky program in the other. The hypothetical final-outcome menus were equivalent. These are group results, not a measurement of every individual's change or coefficient.

**Fitted-parameter context:** the 1992 cumulative paper reports median fitted parameters under a specified functional form, including a loss coefficient about $2.25$. It does not set a universal individual value.

**Linear workshop:** $v(x)=x$ for $x\ge0$ and $v(x)=\lambda x$ for $x<0$, where $x$ is final credits minus the same reference $R$ for every outcome. Compare the certain score $S$ with the probability-weighted risky score $T$ using $D=T-S$. Positive $D$ favors risky under this rule, negative favors certain, and zero is indifference. Probabilities are ordinary; no fees, later plays or additional constraints enter these one-period menu questions.

| Menu | Certain final credits | Risky final credits | Risky probabilities |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Original | 100 | 80 or 130 | 1/2 each |
| Transfer | 100 | 92 or 116 | 1/2 each |

**Additive-play card:** each play adds $-20$ or $+30$ credits with equal probability. Plays are independent unless an item explicitly changes dependence; all selected plays occur, with no fee or stopping rule. A negative total means strictly below zero at the stated endpoint, not any earlier loss.
