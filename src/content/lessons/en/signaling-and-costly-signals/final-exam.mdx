### Cards for the workshop questions

**Card A — one encounter, fictional payoff credits (cr).** H has prior probability 1/4 and L 3/4; equivalently, a stipulated catalogue has one H entry and three L entries of equal weight. The sender knows its type; the receiver sees only certificate or none. Acceptance gives either sender 10 cr, decline zero before signal cost. A certificate costs H 2 and L 12 cr, paid even if declined; none costs zero. Receiver acceptance gives +6 for H and −6 for L; declining gives zero. The certificate does not improve type, both signals are feasible, and there is no reputation or other payoff. The old proposal is H certifies/L does not; beliefs after certificate/none are 1/0; accept certificate/decline none. In the cheaper-L variant **only L cost changes to 6 cr**.

**Card B — separate transfer.** Keep sender rewards and certificate costs from base A. Change the prior to H 3/5 and L 2/5 and the receiver payoff from accepting L to −18 cr; accepting H gives +6 and declining zero. With belief q of H, acceptance gives 6q − 18(1 − q). No tie-break is stipulated.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
A signalling proposal needs more than a costly-looking action. In the common-fixed-premium example, the cost window checks sender incentives under an assumed response. A complete assessment also specifies receiver choices and beliefs, with Bayes consistency at positive-probability observations. In biology this is Zahavi's **handicap principle**: the peacock's tail and the gazelle's **stott** are honest *because* they are costly in a currency only high quality can spare, an **index** (impossible to fake) or a **handicap** (too costly to fake), selected at the level of the **individual**, not "the good of the species." In markets it is Spence's **job-market signalling** (a degree sorts by ability even if it teaches nothing, hence the **sheepskin effect**), and the family of lemons-fixes — **warranties, brands, skin-in-the-game, costly advertising** — plus the **screening** (uninformed builds the test) vs **signalling** (informed pays to reveal) distinction and the **credential-inflation** arms race. In strategy and society it is deterrence by **sinking costs** (pay up front) or **tying hands** (**burn the bridge** so backing down hurts), **conspicuous consumption / Veblen goods**, virtue signalling, and costly **initiation rites** — all one move: an action too costly for the wrong type. The **equilibria** come in three flavours — **pooling** (all the same, receiver keeps the prior), **separating** (types split, beliefs and best responses fit), **semi-separating** (probabilistic, partial reveal) — with **countersignalling** as the twist where the secure top skip the signal (the billionaire in a hoodie). And the model **lies** at its edges: changed imitation costs can invalidate an old proposal without proving a replacement equilibrium; positional races burn **deadweight waste**; **revelation vs causation** is a genuine confound (does the degree reveal or build ability?); over-reading a **noisy one-shot** signal over-fits to luck; and **cheap talk can inform under suitable strategies and incentives**, rather than automatically working or failing. Audit beliefs, receiver best responses and both sender deviations. Specify beliefs after unseen observations; passing a weak check is not a uniqueness or learning prediction.
</Callout>
