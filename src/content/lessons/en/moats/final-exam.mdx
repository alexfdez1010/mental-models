This is the final exam for **Moats & Durable Advantage**. It pulls together everything:
the **excess-return** and entry benchmark, feasible competitive routes with explicit
costs and timing, the **five moats** (network effects, switching costs, scale,
intangibles, efficient scale) and exactly how each is **breached**, the signals (product, head start, team and share) that need explaining, why moats
**erode** and the **Red Queen** truth that you must keep digging, **capital allocation**
to widen a moat, and how to investigate **pricing power** and **persistent returns on capital** as
evidence rather than sufficient proof. Several questions look easy until you spot the trap: calling a hot
product a moat, mistaking fame or share for pricing power, assuming a moat is permanent, or
blessing one good year as durability.

## Data card for the accounts

The first d rounds of horizon T are preparation, with no operations or additional costs. Then q units sell per active round at p cr/unit, with variable cost c cr/unit and fixed cost F cr/active round. Sales, quotes, access and feasible finance are assumptions, not consequences of price. Rival response, other plans, taxes, discounting, financing costs, residual value and uncertainty are omitted. A tie retains both alternatives.

<Callout variant="success" title="Key takeaways">
An **economic moat** can make an advantage difficult for a rival to reproduce or displace.
**Excess return** compares return on capital with its cost; the free-entry benchmark is
not a universal forecast. Investigate feasible routes, costs, access and timing. A label
about a product, team, head start or share does not answer those questions. There are **five real
moats** — **network effects** (often compounding), **switching costs**, **scale / cost
advantage**, **intangible assets** (brand, patents, licenses), and **efficient scale** — each
breached in its own characteristic way, and the strongest businesses stack several. Beware the
**mirages**: a hot product, a first-mover\'s head start, a brilliant team, and market share are
observations that need evidence about entry conditions. A temporary advantage can have
value without becoming permanent protection. **No moat is permanent** — they die by disruption, neglect, market change, and legal
expiry, so the real job is to **widen the moat** through disciplined capital allocation. Investigate **pricing power** and **persistence** alongside costs and alternatives. A
named mechanism needs testing, rather than just a convincing story.
</Callout>
