Two bird species feed in the same tree. If they eat at different heights or at different times, sharing a location need not mean competing for exactly the same food. To understand whether they can coexist, you need to know how each uses resources and how those resources are renewed.

An ecological **niche** describes the conditions and relationships through which a species lives and reproduces. The **competitive exclusion principle** predicts that species competing for the same limiting resources cannot coexist indefinitely under constant conditions when one has a persistent advantage. Resource partitioning can support coexistence, while environmental variation, disturbance and interactions with other species complicate the simple model.

Building on natural selection and the Red Queen effect, you will vary overlap in an interactive niche model, examine specialist and generalist strategies, and trace changes through food webs and keystone species. Business applications ask a related question: which customers and needs overlap? That analogy can suggest ways to investigate a crowded market, but firms can choose rules, cooperate and change offerings in ways the ecological model does not capture. You will practise stating what transfers across domains and what still needs its own evidence.
