Your team chooses a software platform. A year later, its files, training and integrations all depend on that choice. A rival platform may now offer a better feature, but switching means moving the whole working system. Yesterday's decision has changed today's alternatives.

**Path dependence** means that the sequence of past events influences a system's later outcomes. It becomes especially important when an early advantage is reinforced by learning, coordination, setup costs or expectations. **Lock-in** occurs when those accumulated commitments make switching difficult, even if another arrangement would be preferable under some comparison.

This expert course builds on feedback loops and critical mass. You'll identify the mechanisms of increasing returns, examine standards and institutions, and distinguish evidence of lock-in from a story that merely says history mattered. The QWERTY case includes the dispute over claims that the winning layout was inferior. You'll also examine coordinated switching, external shocks and changes in quality. A persistent standard is not automatically a bad one, and a costly path can still be changed.
