A team removes code review to ship a release sooner. The immediate result is less waiting. Later, defects may create support work and interrupt the next release. Whether the shortcut saves time depends on those later consequences and on how likely they are.

**Second-order thinking** follows the consequences of a consequence. It asks how people, resources and systems respond after the first effect. Later effects may reinforce the intended benefit, weaken it or reverse it; their distance from the original decision also makes them harder to predict.

Building on inversion and first-principles thinking, you'll draw consequence trees, connect branches to incentives and feedback, and compare decisions over an explicit time horizon. Worked cases help you distinguish a plausible causal chain from an elaborate story. You'll also practise stopping: prioritise effects that could change the decision, state uncertain assumptions, and look for evidence rather than extending every branch indefinitely. Use the model when the first result is easy to see but the responses to it may matter just as much.
