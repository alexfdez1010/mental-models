A task usually takes four days. Scheduling it to finish at the exact moment the next task begins leaves no room for a late delivery or a failed check. A spare day gives some protection, but whether it is enough depends on the uncertainty and the cost of missing the deadline.

A **margin of safety** is a deliberate buffer between an expected requirement and the limit at which failure becomes unacceptable. In engineering it may involve capacity above anticipated loads; in a plan it may be time or resources left available. A useful margin addresses a particular failure mode rather than simply making every number bigger.

Building on second-order thinking, you'll explore how uncertainty affects the size of a buffer, compare redundancy and fail-safes, and use an interactive meter to see the gap between demand and capacity. You'll also weigh the cost of unused capacity and examine shared causes that can defeat several backups at once. The model helps you reason about resilience; it supplies neither a universal safety factor nor a guarantee against every surprise.
