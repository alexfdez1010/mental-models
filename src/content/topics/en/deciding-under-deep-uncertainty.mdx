A town must choose a water-storage plan that will last for decades. Future demand, rainfall and costs may all change, and different models give different projections. Selecting the plan with the best result under one forecast can leave the town exposed if that forecast misses an important condition.

**Deep uncertainty** arises when the relevant models, probabilities or values are not known well enough, or agreed on well enough, to support a single reliable calculation. An expected value can still be calculated from assumed probabilities; the question is how much the decision depends on assumptions you cannot defend. **Robustness** shifts attention towards acceptable performance across a range of plausible conditions.

Building on fat tails, margins of safety and optionality, you will compare scenarios, minimax regret, redundancy, reversible choices and small experiments. The many-worlds explorer shows trade-offs within its selected scenarios; surviving those scenarios is not a guarantee of surviving every possible future. Extra protection also costs resources and may close useful options. The course helps you state what must be protected, which uncertainties could change the decision and what evidence should trigger a review or a change of course.
