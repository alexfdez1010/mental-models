A service is growing quickly. Social proof suggests that new customers may attract more customers. Capacity constraints suggest that the same growth could overwhelm support. Incentives ask why the growth figures are the ones being reported. Before deciding to expand, you need to connect those explanations and check which evidence each requires.

A **latticework of mental models** brings tools from different disciplines into one judgment. Models can reinforce the same explanation, check a tempting conclusion or describe different parts of a situation. These are useful ways to organise the work, rather than a rule that every case must fit one category. Agreement also needs scrutiny: two models may be naming the same underlying effect.

Building on the lollapalooza effect, second-order thinking and your circle of competence, you will use an interactive decision desk to compare explanations, work through a complete case and build a checklist or pre-mortem. The course concentrates on model selection: what the decision needs, what would change your mind and when you have enough to act. More models help only when they add information or expose an assumption worth testing.
