A team selects the five highest scorers on a test and tests them again. Their average score falls, although no one has become less skilled. If the first score combined ability with temporary luck, selecting the highest scores also selected some unusually lucky performances.

**Regression to the mean** is the tendency for an extreme observation to be followed by a less extreme one when repeated measurements are imperfectly correlated. A signal-plus-noise model makes the mechanism visible: the unusually favourable noise selected on the first test need not recur. This is a tendency in the comparison, not a force that pushes every individual result towards average.

Building on probability and Bayesian updating, you'll work through numerical examples, inspect an interactive scatterplot and distinguish regression from a real trend or the gambler's fallacy. You'll then examine why praise, punishment or an intervention can receive credit for a change that would have occurred anyway. Use comparison groups and repeated measurements to investigate that possibility; regression alone does not establish that an intervention had no effect.
