You need a meeting room for six people, with a screen, available on Tuesday and costing no more than \$100. The first room that meets those conditions might be enough. Spending another afternoon comparing rooms has a cost, even if a slightly better one exists.

**Bounded rationality** describes decision-making with limited time, information and computing capacity. Herbert Simon's **satisficing** is one response: set an **aspiration level**, search for an option that meets it and stop when you find one. That rule requires work upfront. You must decide which conditions matter, whether the threshold is realistic and when to revise it if the search fails.

This course connects satisficing to optimal stopping, the value of information and your circle of competence. It examines maximising and satisficing as decision styles, then tests fast and frugal heuristics against the environments where they work. A simple rule can fail after the environment changes, and choosing an acceptable option does not prove it is the best available one. You will practise reserving detailed comparison for decisions where the expected benefit justifies it, while giving routine choices a defensible stopping point.
