A replacement battery pack seems expensive. Listing its raw materials gives a much lower total. Have you found a cheaper finished pack? Not yet: assembly, testing, control electronics, safety requirements and a supplier's costs may still be missing. Breaking a problem down helps only if you rebuild enough of it to answer the original question.

**First-principles thinking** starts from the facts and constraints relevant to a problem, then reasons towards a solution. **Reasoning by analogy** starts from a similar case. Both can be useful. Analogy saves work when the important conditions match; first-principles reasoning helps examine whether a familiar constraint is real or simply inherited from past choices.

This beginner course builds on what a mental model is. You will compare the two approaches, follow a ladder of “why” questions, separate evidence from assumptions and reconstruct the battery example with an interactive builder. The course also asks when to stop decomposing: a useful starting fact must be justified for the decision, rather than declared unquestionable. You will practise choosing the depth of analysis by what is uncertain, what a mistake would cost and whether the proposed solution can be checked.
