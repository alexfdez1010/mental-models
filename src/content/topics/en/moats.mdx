A rival can copy a software feature. It may be harder to copy a network of users, years of integrations or the training a customer would need to replace the product. Those differences affect how long an advantage can survive competition.

An **economic moat** is a structural feature that helps defend a business's competitive advantage. Possible sources include network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, intangible assets and markets that cannot profitably support many competitors. The question is which mechanism protects returns, not whether the company currently has a popular product.

Building on game theory and comparative advantage, you'll examine each source, compare apparent advantages with durable ones, and explore how technology or changing customer needs can erode protection. An interactive model relates competitive pressure to the persistence of returns. You'll also consider evidence such as pricing power and return on capital, while checking alternative explanations. A large market share or a profitable year does not establish a moat, and even a real moat does not establish what a business is worth at a particular price.
