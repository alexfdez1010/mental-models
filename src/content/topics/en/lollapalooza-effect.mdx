At an auction, a bidder sees others competing, hears that the lot is the last available, and has already announced a determination to win. Social proof, scarcity and commitment may reinforce the urge to keep bidding. Understanding any one cue alone may miss how the situation is pulling the decision together.

Charlie Munger used **Lollapalooza effect** for unusually strong outcomes produced by several psychological tendencies acting together. It is a practical label for possible interactions, not a formula proving that every added bias multiplies an effect by a known amount. To explain a case, you need to show how the pressures connect.

Building on confirmation bias, incentives and loss aversion, you'll analyse auctions, market enthusiasm and high-pressure persuasion, and explore an illustrative bias stack. You'll then compare ways to interrupt the process: a prior limit, an independent view, a pause or a written decision check. These are safeguards to evaluate, not guaranteed protection. The model can be misused after the event by attaching bias names to any surprising result; opposing pressures and alternative explanations deserve examination too.
