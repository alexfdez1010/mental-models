A fragile cup can survive a bumpy journey, but the bumps do not improve it. A small experiment works differently: a failed trial can cost little while revealing how to make the next attempt better. The useful question is how a particular system responds to a specified kind and amount of stress.

**Antifragility** describes benefiting from variability or disorder within a range. It differs from **robustness**, which means withstanding the disturbance. Building on asymmetry, fat tails and margins of safety, this course connects that distinction to the curvature of payoffs: how the consequences of larger changes compare with those of smaller ones. The interactive fragility tester lets you compare response curves and examine the point beyond which an apparently beneficial stress becomes damaging.

You will study limited exposure through small experiments, buffers and the barbell approach, then examine **via negativa**: improving a system by removing a harmful exposure, dependency or intervention. Removal also has consequences to check. Neither stress nor subtraction is automatically beneficial; a system's gains may come at the expense of its parts, and surviving a shock does not prove the shock helped. The aim is to identify the response and its limits before choosing what to expose or remove.
