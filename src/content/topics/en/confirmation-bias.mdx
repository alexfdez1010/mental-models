You think a rule accepts even numbers increasing by two. The triples 2, 4, 6 and 8, 10, 12 both pass. But they also fit a broader rule: any three numbers in increasing order. Another similar triple will not tell those explanations apart. Trying 1, 3, 8 would.

**Confirmation bias** is the tendency to favour information that supports an existing belief, through what you search for, how you interpret it or what you remember. The important question in the number task is what a test can distinguish. A passing result is weak support for your particular explanation when many alternatives predict the same result.

The interactive Wason task lets you choose tests before committing to a rule. From there, the course follows confirmation bias into disagreement, selective information and the influence of incentives. You will practise considering the opposite, stating an opposing argument fairly and recording what evidence would change your mind. These habits make beliefs easier to examine; they do not require treating every objection as equally strong. The aim is to evaluate the evidence by its ability to separate explanations, including the one you prefer.
