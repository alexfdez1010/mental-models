A bakery orders more flour when its morning stock runs low. The mill responds to orders from many bakeries, while suppliers adjust to their own costs and constraints. A city's food supply involves coordinated work without requiring one person to know every household's plans.

**Spontaneous order** is a wider pattern that emerges from interactions without being designed in full by a single planner. The **knowledge problem** concerns information dispersed among people: local circumstances, changing needs and practical knowledge that are hard to collect centrally. Prices can communicate some of that information and change people's incentives to act.

Building on supply and demand, emergence and incentives, you'll trace these connections through the pencil example, markets, footpaths and other forms of emergent coordination. You'll also examine the limits. Decentralised outcomes depend on rules and institutions; external costs, market power and missing information can prevent them from serving shared goals. Use the model to ask which decisions require local knowledge, what signals transmit it and where deliberate coordination is still needed.
