A supplier promises delivery by Friday. A customer worries that another order will take priority. A contract with a meaningful late-delivery penalty can change that expectation: delivering late now carries a cost that was absent from the promise alone. Whether it works depends on the penalty, enforcement and the supplier's ability to deliver.

A **credible commitment** changes future choices or their consequences so that others have reason to expect you to follow through. Restricting your options can influence someone else's best response. The restriction must be understood and believable; announcing that you are committed does not establish either condition.

Building on Nash equilibrium and mechanism design, you will use a two-stage game to compare empty threats with commitments supported by incentives. The course separates promises, **deterrence** that discourages an action, and **compellence** that seeks to make someone act. It also examines brinkmanship, second-strike capability and everyday commitment devices. Each application returns to the same checks: what can each side observe, what remains rational later and what happens if the situation changes? A commitment can support cooperation or leave you trapped in an outcome that neither side wants.
