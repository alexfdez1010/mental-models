A fair coin offers a gain of €110 on heads and a loss of €100 on tails. The expected monetary gain is €5, but that calculation alone does not tell you how someone values the risk. A possible loss may weigh more heavily than a gain of the same size, and affordability matters too.

**Loss aversion** describes greater sensitivity to losses than equivalent gains relative to a reference point. It is part of **prospect theory**, developed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky to describe choices under risk. The strength of the asymmetry varies by person, task and context; “losses count twice” is not a universal constant.

Building on probability and incentives, you'll explore the value function, changes in reference point, framing, the endowment effect and probability weighting. Interactive examples let you compare equivalent descriptions before judging a choice. You'll also practise widening the frame and separating past costs from the decision now available. Use the model to examine how a choice is represented, while keeping genuine constraints and reasonable attitudes to risk in view.
