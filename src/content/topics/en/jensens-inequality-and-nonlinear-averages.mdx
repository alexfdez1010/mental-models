An average batch of three jobs does not necessarily have the average processing work. If the work rule is nonlinear, applying it before or after averaging can give different answers. Jensen’s inequality orders those quantities when a response satisfies a precise shape condition on the relevant domain.

This course builds the finite weighted version from its two-point chord definition. You will calculate both operations, identify equality and strictness, construct a mean-preserving conditional spread, and see why variance alone cannot rank every convex response. A final batch-plan audit adds coordination work before comparing complete feasible alternatives.

## What the model helps you check

You can use the method when someone replaces varying inputs with a typical input before applying a cost, benefit or other response rule. The procedure makes the data, domain, units and objective explicit. It does not call every curvature pattern risk or assume that a larger response is desirable.

Examples are constructed and exactly reproducible. The only prerequisite is [Thinking in Probabilities](/thinking-in-probabilities). Convexity, finite support, variance and conditional means are defined here; no calculus or financial background is required.
