People complain about waiting for a lift. One response is to make it move faster. Another is to ask which part of the wait is frustrating: uncertainty about arrival, crowding, or time spent with nothing to do. A different question can reveal alternatives that a speed calculation alone would miss.

**Lateral thinking** deliberately changes the framing or starting assumptions of a problem to generate possibilities. Edward de Bono's techniques include challenging a presumed constraint, using a provocation and introducing an unrelated starting point. These methods help create candidates; they do not establish that a candidate will work.

Building on first-principles thinking and inversion, you'll distinguish an actual restriction from a convention, generate alternatives before selecting one, and use an interactive reframing exercise. You'll then apply ordinary careful reasoning to check feasibility and consequences. Reach for lateral thinking when repeated effort keeps producing variations of the same answer. If the difficulty is a calculation, a missing fact or a genuine physical limit, changing the question may be less useful than resolving that difficulty directly.
