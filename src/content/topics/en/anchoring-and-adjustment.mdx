A seller lists a second-hand bicycle for \$600. You think that is too much and offer \$450. You have moved away from the asking price, but the negotiation still revolves around it. Would you have offered the same amount if you had first compared similar bicycles without seeing that price?

**Anchoring** is the influence of a starting value on a later judgment. An anchor can contain useful information, or it can be arbitrary. The problem is giving it weight it has not earned. **Insufficient adjustment** is one explanation: you move away from the starting value but stop while your estimate is still pulled towards it. The course also examines selective accessibility, where an anchor makes matching information easier to retrieve.

Through estimates, price comparisons and negotiation scenarios, you will practise establishing a range before hearing someone else's number, looking for evidence against an anchor and separating an asking price from an independent valuation. Thinking in probabilities provides the language for uncertainty; incentives help you ask who benefits from setting the starting point. These checks reduce reliance on the anchor without promising that knowing the bias makes you immune to it.
