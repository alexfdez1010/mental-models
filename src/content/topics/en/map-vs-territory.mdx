Two metro stations look close on the diagram. That helps you follow the line, but it may be a poor guide to walking distance. The diagram preserves connections while changing distances and angles; its usefulness depends on what you ask of it.

**The map is not the territory** means that a representation and the reality it describes are different things. Every model selects relationships and leaves details out. A simplification can serve its purpose accurately without being complete, while a detailed map can still be outdated or unsuitable for your decision.

Building on the first course, you'll compare maps with the situations they describe, examine the idea that imperfect models can still be useful, and explore what happens when conditions change. You'll also meet **reification**: treating an abstraction as if it were the thing itself. Interactive examples let you inspect what survives as detail is removed. Use the distinction to name a model's purpose, identify a consequential omission and decide what observation would make you revise it. It does not require abandoning maps; it requires checking their fit.
