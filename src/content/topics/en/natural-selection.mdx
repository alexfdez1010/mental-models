Imagine a population of green and brown beetles on dark ground. Birds spot the green beetles more easily. If colour is inherited and brown beetles leave more offspring, the next generation can contain a larger share of brown beetles. No individual beetle needs to decide to change colour.

**Natural selection** is a change in the prevalence of heritable traits caused by differences in reproductive success. The three ingredients are variation, inheritance and differences in survival or reproduction that affect how many offspring are left. **Fitness** means reproductive success in a particular environment; a trait that helps in one setting can hinder in another.

You need no biology background beyond the first course's idea of a mental model. You'll use a beetle simulator to examine selection pressure, distinguish fitness from strength, and test where the pattern transfers to products, technologies and ideas. Such comparisons need care: companies make deliberate choices, and ideas can change as they spread. Selection has no future goal, and it is one of several processes that shape evolution. Recognising a useful adaptation is the beginning of an explanation, not proof of how it arose.
