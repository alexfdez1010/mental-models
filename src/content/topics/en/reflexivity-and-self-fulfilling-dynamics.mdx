Depositors hear that a bank may struggle to pay withdrawals. They withdraw earlier than they otherwise would, increasing the cash the bank must find immediately. Their response can worsen the very liquidity problem they fear, even if the bank's assets exceed its liabilities.

**Reflexivity** is a two-way relationship between beliefs about a situation and the situation itself, mediated by people's actions. A **self-fulfilling prophecy** helps bring about its predicted outcome. A **self-defeating prophecy** prompts action that prevents it, such as a congestion warning that diverts enough drivers to keep the road clear.

This expert course combines feedback loops, supply and demand, and critical mass. You'll identify the steps in a belief–action–outcome loop, explore market and social examples, and use a simulator to test how feedback strength changes the result. The key test is causal: what action changes which condition? A belief does not make anything possible, and a price change alone does not demonstrate reflexivity. External constraints, available resources and institutional safeguards still shape what happens.
