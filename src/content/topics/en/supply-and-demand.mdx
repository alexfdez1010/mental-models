A frost damages a coffee harvest. At the old price, sellers can offer less coffee while buyers still want roughly the same amount. A simple supply-and-demand model predicts pressure for a higher price, if other conditions remain unchanged.

**Demand** describes the quantity buyers are willing and able to buy at different prices. **Supply** describes the quantity sellers are willing and able to sell. In the basic competitive model, their intersection is an **equilibrium**: the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. A change in price moves a quantity along a curve; a change in other conditions can shift the curve itself.

This beginner course builds on opportunity cost. You'll draw the curves, find equilibrium, distinguish shifts from movements, and measure **elasticity**, the responsiveness of quantity to price. Interactive charts also show shortages and surpluses under binding price limits. Use these predictions with their conditions attached: market power, search costs, time to adjust and changes in both curves can complicate an actual market. The diagram helps organise an explanation; it does not establish why a particular price changed.
