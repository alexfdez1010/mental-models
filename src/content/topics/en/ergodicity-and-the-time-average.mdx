A fair coin multiplies your wealth by 1.5 on heads and 0.6 on tails. The expected multiplier for one round is 0.5 × 1.5 + 0.5 × 0.6 = 1.05: a 5% expected gain. Yet one head and one tail multiply to 0.9, leaving you 10% below where you started. Averaging the possible multipliers and multiplying realised changes answer different questions.

**Ergodicity** concerns when an average along time matches the corresponding average across an ensemble of possible states or paths. Here the decision concerns repeated growth: the long-run geometric growth factor of this independent fair-coin model is √0.9, about 0.949 per round. Wealth tends towards zero on almost every indefinitely repeated path even though expected wealth grows. In the ideal model it remains positive after any finite number of rounds; practical ruin requires a threshold such as a minimum viable balance.

Building on compounding, fat tails and optionality, you will compare parallel players in an ergodicity engine, distinguish additive from multiplicative changes and vary the fraction exposed to each bet. Expected log growth leads to the **Kelly criterion** under specified assumptions. You will examine absorbing barriers, insurance and estimation error as well as the model's limits: expected value remains correct for the quantity it computes, and neither addition nor multiplication alone establishes every ergodicity claim.
