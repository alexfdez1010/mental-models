A forecast gives rain a 30% chance tomorrow. That leaves room for rain and for a dry day. Whether to carry an umbrella depends on that chance, the inconvenience of carrying it and the cost of getting wet. A probability becomes useful when it informs a choice.

**Thinking in probabilities** means describing uncertainty in degrees rather than treating an uncertain forecast as certain. A **base rate** is the frequency of an event in a relevant comparison group. **Expected value** weights possible outcomes by their probabilities. A range helps show uncertainty that a single precise number may hide.

This course starts from the idea of a mental model and builds those tools with worked examples, a base-rate visualiser and an expected-value calculator. You'll compare possibility with probability and judge decisions using the information available when they were made. An unlikely outcome can still happen after a sound decision. Numbers also need support: choosing a poor comparison group or inventing an exact probability can make an estimate look more reliable than it is.
