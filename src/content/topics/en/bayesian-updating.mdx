In a batch of 1,000 items, 10 are defective. A scanner flags all 10, but also flags 99 sound items. An item with a flag is therefore one of 109 flagged items, of which only 10 are defective: about 9.2%. Detecting every defect does not make every positive result a defect.

**Bayesian updating** combines what you knew before an observation with how likely that observation is under competing explanations. Your **prior** is the starting probability; the **likelihood** describes how probable the evidence is under each possibility; the **posterior** is the updated probability. In the scanner example, the low defect rate and the false alarms both matter.

Building on **[Thinking in Probabilities](/thinking-in-probabilities)**, you will work through natural-frequency trees, Bayes' theorem, its odds form and likelihood ratios. An interactive calculator lets you vary the base rate and test accuracy to see what changes. You will also examine why counting dependent reports as independent evidence creates false confidence. Updating gives a result conditional on your assumptions; it cannot repair an inappropriate comparison group or a test whose performance you do not know.
