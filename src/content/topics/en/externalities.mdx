A factory and its customer agree on a price. Neighbours also experience noise from the production, but that effect is absent from the price and the factory's decision. The transaction looks complete to buyer and seller while leaving part of its consequences outside their calculation.

An **externality** is an effect of an activity on others that is not fully reflected in the incentives of those choosing it. A negative externality adds a cost; a positive one adds a benefit. In the standard competitive model, unpriced external costs can lead to more activity than the socially efficient level, while unpriced external benefits can lead to less. Identifying the effect and who experiences it comes before choosing a remedy.

Building on supply and demand and the tragedy of the commons, you will separate private from social marginal costs and benefits, calculate deadweight loss and change the gap with an interactive chart. The course compares **Pigouvian taxes and subsidies**, cap-and-trade, regulation and bargaining under the **Coase theorem**. Each requires conditions to examine: measuring harm, defining rights, monitoring behaviour and paying transaction costs. A corrective policy can improve incentives, but neither a tax nor a private agreement removes those practical constraints.
