You are preparing a workshop. “Make it successful” leaves many possible actions. “How could we make it unusable?” produces a more concrete list: send the wrong link, assume knowledge participants lack, or leave no time to practise. Each failure suggests a check you can perform before the workshop starts.

**Inversion** approaches a problem by considering its opposite or reasoning backwards from an unwanted outcome. For planning, it helps turn a broad goal into failure paths and safeguards. A **premortem** asks you to imagine that a project has failed, then work backwards to plausible causes.

This beginner course builds on the first course's definition of a mental model. You'll compare forward and backward questions, distinguish an actionable safeguard from a vague warning, and practise applying anti-goals to new situations. You'll also learn where inversion stops helping. Removing known failure paths does not by itself create something worthwhile, and imagining a disaster does not establish its probability. Use inversion alongside a positive plan, evidence and a check that the safeguard actually addresses the cause.
