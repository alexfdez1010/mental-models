A graph shows an exposure on the horizontal axis and a measured response on the vertical axis. The response improves at low exposure, then worsens as exposure increases. Extending the first few points in a straight line would miss the reversal.

**Hormesis** describes a dose–response pattern in which low exposure stimulates a measured response and higher exposure inhibits it, or the reverse for a harmful endpoint. The curve's meaning depends on what is being measured. A response that rises is not automatically a health benefit, and the pattern must be demonstrated for the particular substance, system and endpoint.

Building on antifragility, natural selection and asymmetry, you'll compare linear, threshold and hormetic curves, explore adaptive responses, and examine magnitude, frequency, duration and recovery. Worked cases also distinguish strong evidence from contested extrapolation. The general model cannot supply a safe dose or establish that a toxin is beneficial. Use it to question assumptions such as “more has the same effect”, then ask what observations support the actual curve and where its uncertainty lies.
