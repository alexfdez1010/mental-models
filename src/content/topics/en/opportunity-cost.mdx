You have one free evening. A meeting has no entry fee, but attending means giving up the walk you had planned with a friend. Calling it free leaves out something that matters to your decision.

**Opportunity cost** is the value of the best feasible alternative you give up when you choose. The word **best** matters: you do not add together every activity you could have done. Compare the options you can actually take, using the same time period and accounting for money, time and other resources without counting the same cost twice.

This course starts from the idea of a mental model and applies the comparison to study, work, savings and product decisions. You'll distinguish explicit payments from implicit costs, identify the scarce resource behind a choice, and work through numerical cases before testing common traps. Use it when a proposal looks attractive on its own. Its value becomes clearer once you name what you would do instead, including the option of doing nothing.
