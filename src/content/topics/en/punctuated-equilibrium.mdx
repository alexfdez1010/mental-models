A sequence of fossils shows a form staying broadly similar through many layers, followed by the appearance of a different form. Was change truly abrupt, did it occur elsewhere, or is part of the record missing? The shape of the observations needs an explanation.

**Punctuated equilibrium**, developed by Niles Eldredge and Stephen Jay Gould, describes evolutionary patterns with long periods of relative morphological stability and comparatively rapid change associated with speciation. **Stasis** is that relative stability. Rapid on a geological timescale can still mean many generations; the model does not claim that complex organisms appear in a single jump.

Building on natural selection, fitness landscapes and path dependence, you'll examine what fossil gaps can and cannot tell us, how constraints can sustain stability, and why change need not follow a smooth curve. You'll then compare the pattern with technological and organisational change. Those are analogies to investigate, not proof that every stable system stores pressure or must eventually jump. The useful questions are what sustains the observed stability and what evidence would distinguish competing accounts of its end.
