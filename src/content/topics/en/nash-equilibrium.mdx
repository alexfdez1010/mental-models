Two shops would both earn more if neither offered a discount. Yet if one keeps its price high while the other discounts, it loses customers. Before predicting their prices, you need to ask what each shop would choose given the other's choice.

A **Nash equilibrium** is a combination of strategies in which no player can improve their payoff by changing their own strategy alone, while the others keep theirs fixed. Each is playing a **best response** to the others. This gives you a test to apply to a payoff table; it does not establish that the outcome is fair, efficient or likely to be reached.

Building on game theory basics, you'll find mutual best responses in the prisoner's dilemma, compare coordination games with several equilibria, and use matching pennies to understand why some games require randomised strategies. You'll also examine how changing rewards or rules changes the equilibria. Use the model when people's choices depend on one another, and check what it leaves out: incomplete information, learning and mistakes can prevent real play from settling at the predicted point.
