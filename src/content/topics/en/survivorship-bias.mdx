You interview ten founders whose companies survived and find that eight took a large early risk. Does that show the risk helped? You still need to know how many unsuccessful founders took the same risk, and how many companies started altogether.

**Survivorship bias** occurs when conclusions drawn from the cases that remain visible overlook cases removed by a selection process. The surviving sample may answer a different question from the one you meant to ask. That filter can distort estimates of performance and make shared habits look like causes of success.

Building on probability and the availability heuristic, you'll trace missing data in companies, funds and old buildings, and work through the logic behind the bomber example associated with Abraham Wald. You'll learn to identify the selection rule, recover a useful denominator and ask what comparison would support a causal claim. You cannot always recover the missing observations. In those cases, the right outcome may be a narrower conclusion rather than a confident story about unseen failures.
