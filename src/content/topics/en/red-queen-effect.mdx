Two delivery firms both cut delivery time from two days to one. Customers receive a faster service, but neither firm has gained an advantage over the other on speed. The service improved while the competitive gap stayed the same.

The **Red Queen effect** describes situations in which adaptation by competitors changes the demands of the environment, so continued improvement may be needed to maintain relative position. In biology, **coevolution** occurs when populations exert selection pressures on one another. In strategy, a similar pattern can appear when rivals copy useful improvements.

Starting from natural selection, you'll compare absolute performance with relative advantage, examine host–parasite and predator–prey arms races, and explore business situations where matching a rival becomes costly. An interactive race lets you inspect both competitors rather than just one rising score. You'll also examine the limits: competition does not always erase every gain, and an arms race need not continue forever. Use the model to ask which improvements retain value after others respond, and whether changing the basis of competition is possible.
