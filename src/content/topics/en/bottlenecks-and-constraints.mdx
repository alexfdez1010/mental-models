A workshop can cut 30 pieces an hour, paint 10 and pack 20. With enough demand, materials and stable operation, it cannot deliver more than 10 finished pieces an hour. Buying a faster cutter will not raise that limit. It may simply leave more unfinished pieces waiting for paint.

The painting stage is the **bottleneck**, the capacity constraint limiting this serial process. **Throughput** is the amount the whole process delivers per unit of time. The distinction matters: an impressive improvement in one stage can leave throughput unchanged. Delays, variable demand, rework and changing workloads can also move the constraint, so a neat diagram is a starting model to test against observation.

Building on feedback loops, this course uses interactive pipelines to trace where work accumulates and what happens after a constraint is relieved. Goldratt's **Theory of Constraints** provides a sequence: identify the constraint, use its capacity well, organise other steps around it, add capacity when justified and look again. You will also apply **Amdahl's law** to calculate how an unchanged part limits a speed-up. The practical skill is choosing an improvement by its effect on the system's goal.
