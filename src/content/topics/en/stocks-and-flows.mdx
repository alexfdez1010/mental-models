A tank receives 8 litres a minute and drains 5. Its level rises by 3 litres each minute. Reduce the inflow to 6 and it still rises, now by 1 litre a minute. Less water coming in does not yet mean less water in the tank.

A **stock** is an accumulated quantity measured at a moment, such as litres in a tank or items in a warehouse. A **flow** is a quantity per unit of time, such as litres per minute or deliveries per day. Over a period, the change in a stock equals the total inflow minus the total outflow. The units help you keep them apart.

Building on feedback loops, you'll use an interactive bathtub to connect rates, time and accumulated levels, then apply the distinction to debt, inventories and other accumulating systems. You'll examine why stocks retain the effects of earlier flows, how delays contribute to overshoot, and what has to change for a stock to stabilise. Check the system boundary too: an omitted inflow or outflow can make correct arithmetic describe the wrong system.
