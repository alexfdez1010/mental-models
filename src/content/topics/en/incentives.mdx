A company rewards sales staff for the number of contracts signed. A salesperson can improve that number by selling to customers who are likely to cancel soon. If the company cares about lasting revenue, the reward is tracking only part of the job.

An **incentive** changes the attraction of an action through its expected rewards or costs. Money is one incentive; time, status, approval and avoiding a penalty can matter too. Identifying incentives helps explain choices without assuming that personality or stated intentions tell the whole story.

Starting from the idea of a mental model, you'll read payoff tables, explore how rewards can influence judgment, and distinguish a real goal from a convenient proxy. An interactive model lets you change the reward for a behaviour and inspect the response. You'll also examine cases where external rewards undermine other motivations and where a rule encourages gaming. Incentives influence behaviour rather than determining it completely. Use the model to ask what a rule makes worthwhile, whose perspective you are considering and what responses you may have missed.
