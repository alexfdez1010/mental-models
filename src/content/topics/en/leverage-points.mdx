A warehouse keeps missing deliveries. Adding staff may help, but only if labour is the constraint. Better stock information, different ordering rules or a change in the promised delivery time could affect the same problem through different mechanisms.

A **leverage point** is a place in a system where an intervention can produce a substantial change in behaviour. Donella Meadows' framework compares changes to parameters, buffers, feedback, information, rules, goals and underlying assumptions. It encourages you to examine the structure producing a problem before repeatedly adjusting the most visible number.

This advanced course builds on feedback loops and stocks and flows. You'll compare interventions in one system, explore an illustrative leverage ladder and ask how a proposed change reaches the outcome. The ranking is a guide to investigation, not a universal calculation of impact: changing a goal can fail, and a parameter change can matter greatly near a threshold. Consider implementation cost, resistance, delay and direction. A powerful intervention aimed the wrong way can make the original problem worse.
