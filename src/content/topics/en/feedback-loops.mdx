The shower feels cold, so you turn the hot-water control up. Before the warmer water reaches you, you turn it again. Now it is too hot. The action was a response to a real gap, but the delay meant the second adjustment used information that no longer reflected the first.

A **feedback loop** occurs when an effect feeds back to influence the process that produced it. A **reinforcing loop** amplifies a change, including a decline. A **balancing loop** opposes a change or reduces a gap. These names describe direction, rather than whether an outcome is good or bad. A balancing loop can oscillate when its response is delayed or too strong; reinforcement can slow as resources run short.

Building on second-order thinking, you will trace causal links, distinguish stocks from flows and use an interactive loop simulator to vary response and delay. The course links those structures to growth, correction and overshoot before examining possible interventions. A real system can contain several loops acting on different timescales. You will practise stating which loop is likely to dominate, checking that claim against observations and revising the model when the behaviour changes.
