A design search accepts only changes that improve its current score. It climbs to a point where every small permitted change makes the score worse. The search has reached a **local optimum**. A better design may exist elsewhere, but this rule cannot reach it if every available route first requires a worse score.

A **fitness landscape** represents possible variants as locations and their performance in an environment as height. In biology, fitness concerns reproductive success under particular conditions. In engineering or optimisation, the height might be a chosen objective. That makes the landscape a useful representation, while the meaning of distance, height and an allowed step must be specified for each application.

Building on natural selection and the Red Queen effect, you will vary ruggedness in an interactive landscape, compare local search with exploration and examine mutation, drift and recombination. Evolution is not restricted to accepting only uphill steps, and real design spaces have many dimensions that a landscape picture compresses. You will also let the environment change and observe how a previous peak loses value. The practical question is which search rules and constraints keep a process near its current solution, and what evidence would justify exploring somewhere else.
