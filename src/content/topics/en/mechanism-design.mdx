Two people want to share a cake. If one cuts and the other chooses, the cutter has a reason to make portions that seem equally valuable to them: they cannot choose which one they keep. The rule changes the incentives before either person acts.

**Mechanism design** starts with an outcome or property you want and asks which rules could support it when participants have their own information and interests. **Incentive compatibility** concerns whether reporting private information truthfully is in each participant's interest under the mechanism's assumptions. It does not mean that everyone cooperates on every dimension or that enforcement is unnecessary.

Building on Nash equilibrium and incentives, you'll work through cut-and-choose and a second-price auction, examine the revelation principle and explore taxes, deposits and matching rules. You'll also study limits imposed by participation, information and impossibility results. Different results have different assumptions; they do not reduce to a single claim that good rules are impossible. Use the model to test deviations, specify the objective and identify which requirement a practical design must compromise.
