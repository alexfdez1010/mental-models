Two neighbouring cafés are deciding whether to discount lunch. A discount may attract customers if the other café keeps its price, but reduce both cafés' margins if both copy it. Evaluating the discount requires more than calculating its effect in isolation.

**Game theory** studies decisions in which each player's outcome depends on the choices of others. A **payoff matrix** records the outcomes of combinations of choices. A **dominant strategy** performs at least as well regardless of what the other player does; a **Nash equilibrium** is a combination where no one benefits from changing strategy alone.

Building on incentives and second-order thinking, you'll read payoff tables, find best responses and use the prisoner's dilemma to understand how individually attractive choices can produce a worse shared result. You'll compare zero-sum and positive-sum situations, then examine repetition, reputation and cooperation. The model's predictions depend on the payoffs, information and rules you put into it. Describing a situation as a game does not mean that everyone sees the same table or calculates perfectly.
