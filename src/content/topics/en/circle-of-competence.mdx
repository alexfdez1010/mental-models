You use an app every day and can explain its features to a friend. Could you also judge whether its business will remain profitable? Recognising the product gives you a starting point, but the second question needs knowledge about costs, customers and competitors that daily use may never reveal.

Your **circle of competence** covers the questions you understand well enough to judge with some reliability. Its boundary depends on the task: you may be able to repair a bicycle brake while needing help to assess damage to the frame. The feeling that a subject is familiar is weaker evidence than being able to explain a mechanism, make a prediction and learn from the result.

Building on map versus territory, you will practise identifying that boundary, recording predictions and looking for feedback that exposes gaps. The course also examines how to widen the circle through study and deliberate practice, or use the “too hard” category when the decision can wait. If you must act outside your expertise, the model points towards advice, smaller commitments and explicit uncertainty. It helps you decide where your judgment needs support; it cannot remove uncertainty from the areas you know well.
