A shop offers a free sample, displays a best-seller label and says that an offer ends tonight. You may have good reasons to buy, but each cue can change how the offer feels before you have compared its price or usefulness.

This course examines **influence and persuasion** through the principles described by Robert Cialdini: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity and unity. These are patterns that can affect decisions under particular conditions, not buttons that reliably control another person.

Building on incentives and confirmation bias, you'll identify what each cue invites you to infer, why that inference can sometimes be useful, and how it can be exploited. Worked scenarios and an illustrative compliance meter let you compare combinations and possible resistance. The meter represents a teaching model rather than a measured probability for a real customer. You'll finish by examining consent, honest communication and practical checks, such as comparing the offer again without the deadline or popularity cue. Knowing a principle helps you inspect a decision; it does not make you immune to influence.
