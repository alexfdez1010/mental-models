After reading three reports about bicycle theft, you feel your own bicycle is much less safe. Before changing your plans, you need to ask whether theft has increased locally, whether the reports describe separate incidents and how many bicycles were at risk. Three memorable reports do not supply those denominators.

The **availability heuristic** is a shortcut that estimates frequency or probability from how easily examples come to mind. A **heuristic** is a practical rule used in place of a fuller calculation. Easy recall can be informative when repeated experience reflects the situation you are judging. It becomes misleading when vividness, recency, emotion or repeated coverage make a case memorable without making it more common.

The course examines each of those influences, the substitution of an easy question for a harder one, and the way shared stories can become availability cascades. You will compare perceived risk with recorded frequency in an interactive chart and practise returning to base rates. The goal is to choose relevant evidence: a vivid account may reveal a previously overlooked problem, but its emotional force cannot tell you how frequent that problem is.
