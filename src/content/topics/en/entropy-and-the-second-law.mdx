A hot coffee cools in a room. Energy leaves the coffee and enters its surroundings. Looking only at the cup misses half the exchange; looking at both lets you ask what changes and why the reverse process does not happen spontaneously at this scale.

**Entropy** is a thermodynamic quantity with a statistical interpretation in terms of possible microscopic arrangements. The **second law of thermodynamics** says that the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease. A part of that system can lose entropy while its surroundings gain enough to compensate. This is why a refrigerator needs a wider accounting than “the inside got colder”.

The course connects microstates and macrostates, the arrow of time, useful work and the **Carnot limit** for a heat engine operating between two temperatures. You will use particle and engine simulations before examining information entropy and the conditions behind the thermodynamic cost of erasing a bit. Familiar images such as a messy room can help introduce an idea, but visual untidiness is not a measurement of entropy. Applying the model to maintenance or organisations requires an explicit analogy; thermodynamics alone cannot prove a social prediction or a rule for managing a business.
