Two articles are equally easy to find at first. One receives an early link. Readers who discover it may add more links, making it easier for the next reader to find. The extra visibility changes the process that allocates future attention, so an initial difference can grow even without a matching difference in quality.

**Cumulative advantage** is a reinforcing process in which having more increases the chance of gaining more. **Preferential attachment** is one model of that process. Under particular rules it can generate a **power-law distribution**, with many small outcomes and a few very large ones. Other mechanisms and distributions can also create unequal outcomes; a large winner or a straight-looking log–log plot is not enough to identify the cause.

Building on fat tails and feedback loops, you will vary attachment strength in an interactive token-allocation engine, inspect rank-size plots and connect the results to the Pareto principle and superstar markets. You will distinguish the 80/20 rule of thumb from a fitted statistical law, and examine how tail exponents affect averages and uncertainty. The practical task is to investigate how gains are allocated before reading a winner's size as a direct measure of skill, or assuming its lead must keep growing.
