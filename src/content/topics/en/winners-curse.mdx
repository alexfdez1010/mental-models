Ten bidders estimate the contents of a sealed jar of coins. Their estimates differ because each has an imperfect view. If everyone bids their estimate, the highest bidder is selected for having the most optimistic estimate, which may exceed the value of the coins.

The **winner's curse** is the risk of overpaying when winning a competition for uncertain common value is itself evidence that your estimate was unusually high. **Common value** means that bidders are estimating a shared underlying value. With purely **private value**, what an item is worth to one bidder need not depend on another bidder's information in the same way.

This expert course combines probability, regression to the mean and mechanism design. You'll examine the selection effect, condition on winning, and compare bidding rules that allow for the information contained in other bids. You'll also explore acquisitions and other mixed-value settings where the analogy needs care. Winning does not prove that you overpaid: bidders can adjust, information can differ in quality, and aggressive discounting can make useful opportunities disappear. The aim is to evaluate the inference before setting a bid.
