In a flocking simulation, each dot keeps some distance from nearby dots, aligns its direction with theirs and moves towards their group. No dot receives a map of the whole flock. Yet a coordinated pattern can form. Change one local rule and the pattern changes with it.

**Emergence** describes patterns or properties of a system that arise from interactions between its parts and are not properties of an isolated part. Understanding the components helps, but you also need their connections and the conditions in which they interact. The flocking model shows one way coordination can arise; it does not establish that real birds follow only those three rules.

Building on natural selection, you will explore the boids simulation and Schelling's segregation grid, where local preferences can produce larger-scale separation. You will compare a pattern that can be derived from the model's rules with stronger philosophical claims about emergence. The practical work is to trace the mechanism before choosing an intervention: changing rules, information or connections may change the group outcome, while effects on individual parts still matter. A surprising result is a reason to inspect the interactions, rather than evidence that no one has influence over the system.
