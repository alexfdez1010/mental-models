Several herders share a pasture. Adding another cow brings one herder extra milk, while the reduced grass affects everyone. If each herder counts only their share of that cost, adding cows can look worthwhile even as the pasture becomes overused.

The **tragedy of the commons** describes this conflict between individual incentives and the sustained use of a shared, limited resource. It is especially clear when access is open and users lack effective rules. A shared resource is not automatically doomed: communities can agree on limits, monitor use and respond when rules are broken.

Building on incentives and supply and demand, you'll work through the pasture example, identify the external costs in other settings, and use a fishery simulator to examine extraction and replenishment. You'll then compare community governance, property rights, quotas, regulation and taxes, including the work of Elinor Ostrom. Each approach has conditions and enforcement costs. Use the model to diagnose the resource and its rules, rather than assuming that either private ownership or goodwill must solve every case.
