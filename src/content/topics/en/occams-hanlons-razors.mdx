A website stops working just after an update. You could investigate the update or invent a story about an attacker who struck at exactly the same moment. Both are possible; they do not require the same number of unsupported assumptions. Start with the update, then let the evidence decide.

A **razor** is a rule of thumb for comparing explanations. **Occam's razor** favours fewer unnecessary assumptions when explanations account for the evidence equally well. **Hanlon's razor** asks you to consider error or carelessness before attributing an action to malice. Neither rule proves a cause or excuses repeated harm.

Building on first-principles thinking, you'll identify hidden assumptions, compare competing explanations and separate a useful starting hypothesis from a settled conclusion. You'll also examine failures: reality can be complex, an apparently simple story can conceal assumptions, and deliberate deception does happen. Reach for these tools when choosing what to investigate first; revise your choice when the evidence calls for it.
