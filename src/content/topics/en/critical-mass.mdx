A message reaches 100 people. In a simple model, each reader brings in an average of 0.8 new readers, so the next rounds reach 80, then 64. Change that average to 1.2 and they reach 120, then 144. The difference is small at each step, but repeated multiplication sends the two paths apart.

**Critical mass** refers to conditions under which a chain reaction becomes self-sustaining. In a simple branching model, an average reproduction factor of 1 marks the boundary between decline and growth. Nuclear fission gives the model its name, but real criticality depends on material, geometry and losses; crossing it does not by itself imply an explosion. Social and economic applications need their own mechanism and evidence.

Building on feedback loops and emergence, you will drive a cascade simulator and compare subcritical, critical and supercritical behaviour. The course examines network effects, adoption curves, nonlinear change and **hysteresis**, where reversing a change may require different conditions from those that triggered it. Finite audiences, depleted resources and changing behaviour can stop growth. The model helps you inspect a possible threshold; a slow start alone cannot show that a breakthrough is about to happen.
