Consider a repeated coin game: heads wins the amount staked, tails loses it, and the known probability of heads is 60%. Staking everything has positive expected profit for one round, yet a single loss ends the sequence. The size of the stake changes what repeated play can produce.

The **Kelly criterion** chooses the fraction of capital that maximises expected logarithmic growth under a specified model. For a win probability $p$, loss probability $q = 1-p$ and net win of $b$ times the stake, the unconstrained fraction is $f^*=(pb-q)/b$. In the 60% even-money example, it is 20%. That is a model result with an explicit objective, not a recommendation for a real person's savings.

Building on ergodicity, probability and compounding, you'll compare growth curves, follow simulated capital paths and examine fractional Kelly. The course shows why stakes above the growth-maximising point reduce modelled growth. It also examines uncertain probabilities, correlated outcomes, finite horizons and drawdowns. A formula fitted to the wrong odds or the wrong objective can give a precise answer to the wrong problem.
