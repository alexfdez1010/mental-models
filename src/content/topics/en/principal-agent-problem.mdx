You hire a contractor to finish a job. Paying by the hour makes extra work profitable; paying only for completion may encourage shortcuts you cannot see. Neither contract tells you, by itself, whether the finished work will meet your needs.

The **principal–agent problem** arises when one party delegates to another whose interests may differ and whose actions or information cannot be fully observed. The **principal** wants an outcome; the **agent** acts on their behalf. Hidden action creates one kind of difficulty, often called **moral hazard**. Hidden information creates another, including **adverse selection** before a contract is signed.

This expert course combines incentives and mechanism design. You'll compare monitoring, performance pay, reputation and screening, account for agency costs, and use an interactive contract designer to examine trade-offs between effort, risk and gaming. A stronger reward can improve a measured result while damaging work the metric misses. The aim is to choose a workable arrangement under stated assumptions, taking trust, motivation and the cost of oversight seriously.
