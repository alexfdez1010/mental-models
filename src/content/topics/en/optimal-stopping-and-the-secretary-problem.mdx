A flat becomes available today, but tomorrow's listings are still unknown. Accept it and the search ends; reject it and someone else may take it. How much should you learn about the market before choosing?

**Optimal stopping** studies decisions about when to act as information arrives. In the classic **secretary problem**, a known number of uniquely ranked options arrives in random order, rejected options cannot be recalled, and only choosing the single best counts as success. For a large number of options, the optimal rule is to reject roughly the first 37%, remember the best, then accept the first later option that beats it. Its success probability approaches 37% too.

You'll derive the rule from small cases and distinguish this particular objective from maximising expected value. Building on probability, information value and uncertainty, the course then examines search costs, known quality distributions, uncertain numbers of options and the ability to return to an earlier offer. The model helps you define a search policy; applying 37% to a real decision without checking these conditions can optimise the wrong goal.
