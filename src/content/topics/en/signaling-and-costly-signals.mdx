Two sellers claim their products are reliable. One offers a warranty that would be expensive to honour if its products failed often. That warranty may convey information, but only if buyers believe the seller can and will honour it, and if less reliable sellers find copying it unattractive.

**Signalling** occurs when an informed party takes an observable action that can affect another party's beliefs. In a **separating equilibrium**, different types choose different signals. A cost gap can support honest signalling when the reward makes sending the signal worthwhile for one type but not the other. High expense on its own proves little.

This expert course connects natural selection, agency problems and credible commitment. You'll examine biological signals, job-market credentials, warranties, pooling and separating outcomes, and countersignalling. An interactive model lets you compare the costs faced by different types. You'll also consider wasteful signalling races and dishonest mimicry. The test is to examine incentives and available evidence; credible communication can also depend on reputation or verifiable information, rather than large spending.
