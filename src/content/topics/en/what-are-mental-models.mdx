A metro map helps you choose a line and a transfer. It usually cannot tell you which exit has stairs or how far apart two stations are on foot. The same diagram can be useful for one decision and insufficient for another.

A **mental model** is a simplified representation of how something works that you use to explain, predict or decide. A thermostat's on–off rule, a map and a diagram of who reports to whom all keep certain relationships and omit others. The skill is to recognise which relationships matter for the question you are trying to answer.

This is the starting course and assumes no prior knowledge. You'll distinguish models from isolated facts, explore the difference between a map and the reality it represents, and connect several models into a **latticework**: a network of tools that can check and complement one another. You'll also meet the **circle of competence**, the boundary between what you can assess reasonably and what you still need to learn. The exercises ask you to use these distinctions in new situations, including cases where an attractive model leaves out the detail that changes the answer.
