Customers move from a local video-rental shop to an online service. They gain a different way to watch films, while the shop loses the sales that pay its staff and rent. The same change creates an opportunity and makes some existing assets less useful. Looking only at the new service misses the cost of that transition.

**Creative destruction**, associated with Joseph Schumpeter, describes innovation reshaping an economy by creating activities while displacing others. Replacement can be partial: old and new technologies may coexist, incumbents may adapt and customers may value different features. A business closing is evidence of a change, not proof that the change benefits society.

This expert course connects supply and demand, feedback loops and competitive moats to examine how challengers grow and why established firms may struggle to respond. You will follow adoption curves, incentives and the reallocation of resources in an interactive wave. The practical questions include who gains, who bears the transition costs, what the new activity makes possible and whether an incumbent can change course. The model helps organise those questions without supplying a timetable for the next industry's decline.
