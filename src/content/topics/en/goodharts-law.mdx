A support team is rewarded for closing tickets quickly. Closing a difficult request before the customer confirms a solution improves the score, while leaving the problem unresolved. The measured result and the intended result have come apart.

**Goodhart's law** warns that turning a measure into a target can weaken its usefulness as a measure. A **proxy** is an observable stand-in for something harder to assess. Once rewards depend on the proxy, people may change what they do, which cases they select or how they record the result. The earlier relationship between the number and the goal may no longer hold.

This expert course combines incentives, feedback loops and principal–agent problems. You'll distinguish several ways metrics break, compare productive work with score inflation in an explicit action menu, and compare safeguards such as complementary measures, independent checks and attention to outcomes. No metric is guaranteed to fail, and measurement remains useful. The practical task is to inspect the mechanism: what does meeting the target encourage, what does it leave unmeasured, and what evidence would reveal the gap?
